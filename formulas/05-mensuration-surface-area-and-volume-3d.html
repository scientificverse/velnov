Cube

Volume of a cube Class 8

V = a³

alength of an edge

Where it is used: To find the space inside a cube — boxes, dice, storage cubes, and volume problems.

In simple words: A cube has equal sides. Multiply one edge by itself three times to find how much it can hold.

Example: a = 4 cm → V = 4³ = 64 cm³

Total surface area of a cube Class 8

TSA = 6 × a²

alength of an edge

Where it is used: To find the total outer area of a cube — how much paint or paper covers all 6 faces.

In simple words: A cube has 6 equal square faces. Find one face's area and multiply by 6.

Example: a = 4 → TSA = 6 × 16 = 96 cm²

Diagonal of a cube

d = a × √3

aedge length
dlongest inside diagonal

Where it is used: To find the longest straight line inside a cube (corner to opposite corner).

In simple words: The longest line through a cube is its edge times about 1.732.

Example: a = 4 → d = 4√3 ≈ 6.93 cm

Lateral surface area of a cube

LSA = 4 × a²

aedge length

Where it is used: To find the area of the four side faces of a cube (not top and bottom).

In simple words: The four walls of a cube — four square faces.

Example: a=5 → 4×25 = 100 cm²

Volume of a cube from diagonal

V = (d ÷ √3)³

dthe long diagonal

Where it is used: To find a cube's volume when only its longest diagonal is known.

In simple words: Get the edge from the diagonal first, then cube it.

Example: d=√3 → edge 1 → V = 1 cm³

Cuboid

Volume of a cuboid Class 8

V = l × b × h

llength
bbreadth
hheight

Where it is used: To find the space inside a box shape — rooms, bricks, tanks, cartons.

In simple words: Multiply length, width and height to find how much a box-shaped thing can hold.

Example: l=5, b=4, h=3 → V = 5×4×3 = 60 cm³

Total surface area of a cuboid Class 8

TSA = 2 × (lb + bh + hl)

llength
bbreadth
hheight

Where it is used: To find the total outer area of a box — wrapping paper, paint for a room's walls and ceiling.

In simple words: A box has 6 rectangular faces in 3 matching pairs. Add the three different faces and double it.

Example: l=5, b=4, h=3 → TSA = 2(20+12+15) = 94 cm²

Diagonal of a cuboid

d = √(l² + b² + h²)

llength
bbreadth
hheight

Where it is used: To find the longest straight line inside a box — packing long items into cartons.

In simple words: Square all three dimensions, add them, and take the square root to get the longest inside line.

Example: l=3, b=4, h=12 → d = √(9+16+144) = 13

Lateral surface area of a cuboid

LSA = 2h(l + b)

llength
bbreadth
hheight

Where it is used: To find the area of the four walls of a room (excluding floor and ceiling) — painting.

In simple words: The area of the four walls of a box-shaped room.

Example: l=5,b=4,h=3 → 2×3×9 = 54 m²

Area of four walls of a room

Area = 2 × height × (length + breadth)

length, breadth, heightroom dimensions

Where it is used: To find how much paint or wallpaper the walls of a room need.

In simple words: Same as lateral surface area — just the four walls.

Example: 5×4 room, 3 high → 2×3×9 = 54 m²

Cylinder

Volume of a cylinder Class 9

V = π × r² × h

rradius of the circular base
hheight

Where it is used: To find the space inside a cylinder — pipes, tanks, tins, glasses, gas cylinders.

In simple words: Find the round base's area (πr²) and multiply by the height, like stacking many circles.

Example: r=7, h=10 → V = 22/7 × 49 × 10 = 1540 cm³

Curved surface area of a cylinder Class 9

CSA = 2 × π × r × h

rradius
hheight

Where it is used: To find the curved (side) area of a cylinder — the label on a tin, paint on a pipe's outside.

In simple words: Unroll the curved side and it becomes a rectangle: its area is the base's circumference times the height.

Example: r=7, h=10 → CSA = 2 × 22/7 × 7 × 10 = 440 cm²

Total surface area of a cylinder

TSA = 2πr(r + h)

rradius
hheight

Where it is used: To find the whole outer area of a closed cylinder — the curved side plus the two circle ends.

In simple words: Add the curved side and the two round lids to get the full outside area.

Example: r=7, h=10 → 2×22/7×7×17 = 748 cm²

Volume of a hollow cylinder (pipe)

V = π × h × (R² − r²)

Router radius
rinner radius
hlength

Where it is used: To find the material volume of a pipe or tube.

In simple words: Subtract the hollow inside from the full outside to get the pipe's material.

Example: R=5,r=3,h=10 → 22/7×10×16 ≈ 502.9 cm³

Cone

Volume of a cone Class 9

V = ⅓ × π × r² × h

rradius of the base
hheight

Where it is used: To find the space inside a cone — ice-cream cones, funnels, conical tents and heaps.

In simple words: A cone holds exactly one-third of the cylinder with the same base and height.

Example: r=3, h=7 → V = ⅓ × 22/7 × 9 × 7 = 66 cm³

Curved surface area of a cone Class 9

CSA = π × r × l

rradius
lslant height

Where it is used: To find the sloping surface area of a cone — the paper of a party hat or the cloth of a conical tent.

In simple words: Multiply pi by the radius and by the slanting side length to get the curved surface.

Example: r=7, l=25 → CSA = 22/7 × 7 × 25 = 550 cm²

Slant height of a cone Class 9

l = √(r² + h²)

lslant height
rradius
hheight

Where it is used: To find the sloping side of a cone from its radius and height, before finding curved surface area.

In simple words: The slant side, the height and the radius form a right triangle, so use Pythagoras to find the slant.

Example: r=3, h=4 → l = √(9+16) = 5

Total surface area of a cone

TSA = πr(l + r)

rradius
lslant height

Where it is used: To find the whole outer area of a solid cone — the sloping side plus the circular base.

In simple words: Add the slanting surface and the round base to get the full outside area.

Example: r=7, l=25 → 22/7×7×32 = 704 cm²

Height of a cone from volume

h = 3V ÷ (π r²)

Vvolume
rradius

Where it is used: To find a cone's height when its volume and radius are known.

In simple words: Rearranges the cone-volume formula to give the height.

Example: V=66, r=3 → h = 198/(22/7×9) = 7

Sphere

Volume of a sphere Class 9

V = 4/3 × π × r³

rradius

Where it is used: To find the space inside a ball-shaped object — balls, globes, bubbles, planets.

In simple words: For a perfectly round ball, cube the radius, multiply by pi, then by four-thirds.

Example: r=3 → V = 4/3 × 22/7 × 27 ≈ 113.1 cm³

Surface area of a sphere Class 9

SA = 4 × π × r²

rradius

Where it is used: To find the outer area of a ball — leather for a football, paint for a dome.

In simple words: The whole surface of a ball is four times the area of the flat circle across its middle.

Example: r=7 → SA = 4 × 22/7 × 49 = 616 cm²

Volume of a sphere from diameter

V = (1/6) × π × d³

ddiameter

Where it is used: To find a sphere's volume directly from its diameter.

In simple words: If you know the full width instead of the radius, use this form.

Example: d=6 → 1/6×22/7×216 ≈ 113.1 cm³

Hemisphere

Volume of a hemisphere Class 9

V = 2/3 × π × r³

rradius

Where it is used: To find the space inside a half-ball — bowls, domes, half-orange shapes.

In simple words: A hemisphere is half a ball, so it holds half the volume of the full sphere.

Example: r=3 → V = 2/3 × 22/7 × 27 ≈ 56.6 cm³

Curved surface area of a hemisphere

CSA = 2πr²

rradius

Where it is used: To find the curved (dome) surface area of a half-ball — bowls, domes.

In simple words: The curved part of half a ball is twice the area of the flat circle across it.

Example: r=7 → 2×22/7×49 = 308 cm²

Total surface area of a hemisphere

TSA = 3πr²

rradius

Where it is used: To find the whole outer area of a solid half-ball — the dome plus its flat circular face.

In simple words: Add the curved dome and the flat circle to get three times the circle's area.

Example: r=7 → 3×22/7×49 = 462 cm²

Prism

Volume of a prism

V = Base area × Height

Base areaarea of the cross-section
Heightlength of the prism

Where it is used: To find the space inside any prism — triangular, hexagonal, or other uniform cross-section.

In simple words: Find the area of the shape at the end and multiply by how long the prism is.

Example: triangular base 6 cm², length 10 → V = 60 cm³

Lateral surface area of a prism

LSA = Perimeter of base × Height

Perimeterof the base shape
Heightlength of the prism

Where it is used: To find the side surface area of a prism.

In simple words: Unroll the sides — their area is the base's perimeter times the length.

Example: base perimeter 12, length 10 → 120 cm²

Frustum

Volume of a frustum of a cone

V = ⅓ × π × h × (R² + r² + Rr)

Rlarger radius
rsmaller radius
hheight

Where it is used: To find the space inside a cone with the tip cut off — buckets, glasses, lampshades.

In simple words: A bucket shape: this gives its capacity from the two circle radii and the height.

Example: R=6,r=3,h=7 → ⅓×22/7×7×(36+9+18) = 462 cm³

Curved surface area of a frustum

CSA = π × l × (R + r)

lslant height
R, rthe two radii

Where it is used: To find the sloping surface area of a bucket-shaped frustum.

In simple words: The slanting side area of a cut-off cone.

Example: R=6,r=3,l=5 → 22/7×5×9 ≈ 141.4 cm²
