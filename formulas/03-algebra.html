Identities

Square of a sum Class 8

(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²

a, bany numbers or terms

Where it is used: To expand or quickly square a sum, and to simplify algebraic expressions and mental-maths squaring.

In simple words: Squaring a two-part sum gives each part squared plus twice their product.

Example: (x+3)² = x² + 6x + 9

Square of a difference Class 8

(a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²

a, bany numbers or terms

Where it is used: To expand the square of a difference in algebra and to square numbers like 99 easily.

In simple words: Squaring a two-part difference gives each part squared minus twice their product.

Example: (x−2)² = x² − 4x + 4

Difference of two squares Class 8

a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b)

a, bany numbers or terms

Where it is used: To factorise a difference of squares — very common in simplification and solving equations.

In simple words: One square minus another equals the sum times the difference of the two numbers.

Example: x² − 9 = (x+3)(x−3)

Product identity (x+a)(x+b) Class 9

In simple words: Multiply out the brackets: the middle number is the sum of a and b, the last is their product.

Example: (x+2)(x+3) = x² + 5x + 6

In simple words: This one number tells you whether the equation has two answers, one, or none, before you solve it.
