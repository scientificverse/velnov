Q1. easy

The Vedic Age is named after which body of texts?

  1. A The Vedas
  2. B The Puranas
  3. C The Upanishads only
  4. D The Tripitakas

Answer: A — The Vedas

The period is named after the Vedas, the oldest religious texts of the Aryans and the primary source for reconstructing this age.

VedasAryans

Q3. easy

How many Vedas are there?

  1. A Four
  2. B Three
  3. C Two
  4. D Eight

Answer: A — Four

There are four Vedas: Rigveda, Samaveda, Yajurveda and Atharvaveda. The Rigveda is the oldest.

Four Vedas

Medium questions (34)

Q2. medium

The word 'Veda' is derived from the Sanskrit root 'vid', which means:

  1. A To know (knowledge)
  2. B To fight
  3. C To pray
  4. D To trade

Answer: A — To know (knowledge)

'Veda' comes from 'vid' meaning 'to know'. The Vedas are regarded as 'apaurusheya' (not of human origin) and 'shruti' (that which is heard).

ShrutiKnowledge

Q4. medium

Which is the OLDEST Veda (and the oldest text in the world in continuous use)?

  1. A Rigveda
  2. B Samaveda
  3. C Yajurveda
  4. D Atharvaveda

Answer: A — Rigveda

The Rigveda is the oldest Veda, a collection of hymns in praise of gods. It is one of the oldest religious texts still in use.

RigvedaOldest text

Q6. medium

Which Veda deals with music and melodies (the origin of Indian classical music)?

  1. A Samaveda
  2. B Rigveda
  3. C Yajurveda
  4. D Atharvaveda

Answer: A — Samaveda

The Samaveda contains hymns (mostly borrowed from the Rigveda) set to melodies for chanting during sacrifices. It is regarded as the root of Indian classical music.

SamavedaMusic

Q7. medium

The Yajurveda mainly deals with:

  1. A Sacrificial formulae and rituals
  2. B Melodies
  3. C Magic and charms
  4. D Philosophy only

Answer: A — Sacrificial formulae and rituals

The Yajurveda contains the prose formulae (yajus) and procedures for performing sacrifices. It has two branches: Krishna (Black) and Shukla (White) Yajurveda.

YajurvedaSacrifice

Q8. medium

The Atharvaveda is notable for containing:

  1. A Charms, spells and remedies for everyday life and ailments
  2. B Only hymns to Indra
  3. C Rules of grammar
  4. D Astronomy tables

Answer: A — Charms, spells and remedies for everyday life and ailments

The Atharvaveda contains magical spells, charms and remedies against diseases and enemies, reflecting popular beliefs. It was added later and includes non-Aryan elements.

AtharvavedaCharms

Answer: A — Noble / of good family (not a race)

'Arya' meant 'noble' or 'of high status' and referred to a group speaking an Indo-Aryan language and sharing a culture - not a racial category as later misused.

Q14. medium

The Early Vedic economy was primarily:

  1. A Pastoral (cattle-rearing), with cattle as the main measure of wealth
  2. B Based on large-scale trade and coinage
  3. C Industrial
  4. D Based on maritime commerce

Answer: A — Pastoral (cattle-rearing), with cattle as the main measure of wealth

The Rigvedic Aryans were mainly pastoralists; cattle were the chief form of wealth. Many words (like 'gomat' - rich in cattle) reflect this cattle-centred economy.

Q21. medium

The basic unit of Rigvedic society was the:

  1. A Kula (family)
  2. B Nagara (city)
  3. C Janapada (state)
  4. D Rashtra (nation)

Answer: A — Kula (family)

Society was organised in a kinship hierarchy: Kula (family) → Grama (village) → Vish (clan) → Jana (tribe/people). The family was the basic patriarchal unit.

KulaKinship

  3. C Chakravartin

Answer: A — Rajan (tribal chief)

The Rajan was the tribal chief, more a protector of the tribe and cattle than a territorial king. He was often chosen and assisted by tribal assemblies.

RajanTribal chief

The Purohita (priest) advised the Rajan and performed sacrifices for his success. Vasistha and Vishwamitra were the most famous Rigvedic priests.

Q39. medium

  2. B The Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha

As kingship became hereditary and territorial, the Sabha and Samiti lost power. The Vidatha disappeared. Royal authority was reinforced by elaborate sacrifices.

Q43. medium

The prose texts that explain the rituals and sacrifices of the Vedas are called:

  1. A Brahmanas
  2. B Aranyakas
  3. C Upanishads
  4. D Puranas

Answer: A — Brahmanas

The Brahmanas are prose commentaries attached to each Veda, detailing the meaning and procedure of sacrifices (e.g. Satapatha Brahmana, Aitareya Brahmana).

BrahmanasRitual texts

Q45. medium

The philosophical texts that discuss the soul (Atman), ultimate reality (Brahman) and are the concluding part of the Vedas are the:

  1. A Upanishads (also called Vedanta)
  2. B Brahmanas
  3. C Samhitas
  4. D Vedangas

Answer: A — Upanishads (also called Vedanta)

The Upanishads, the last part of the Vedas, are called 'Vedanta' (end of the Vedas). They explore Atman, Brahman, karma and moksha, forming the basis of Indian philosophy.

UpanishadsVedanta

Q64. medium
