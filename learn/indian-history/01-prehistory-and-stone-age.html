Topic overview

Prehistory and the Stone Age – before writing began

In this overview: Prehistory, protohistory and history · The three Stone Ages · Paleolithic Age (Old Stone Age) · Mesolithic Age (Middle Stone Age) · Neolithic Age (New Stone Age) · Chalcolithic Age (Copper-Stone Age) · Common misconceptions · Quick revision · Test your understanding

1. Prehistory, protohistory and history

  • Prehistory is the period before writing. We know it only from material remains: tools, bones, pottery and rock paintings.
  • Protohistory is a period that had writing we cannot yet read, e.g. the Harappan civilization.
  • History is the period with written records we can read.

Archaeology is the study of the past through remains dug from the ground.

Example: Robert Bruce Foote found the first Paleolithic tool in India at Pallavaram, near Chennai, in 1863. He is called the Father of Indian Prehistory.

2. The three Stone Ages

The Stone Age is divided by the kind of tools people used:

Paleolithic (Old) → Mesolithic (Middle) → Neolithic (New)

Remember: “Paleo” = old, “meso” = middle, “neo” = new, and “lithic” = stone.

3. Paleolithic Age (Old Stone Age)

The Paleolithic Age lasted from about 2 million years ago to c. 10,000 BCE, during the Ice Age (the Pleistocene epoch). People were nomadic hunter-gatherers living in caves and rock shelters.

  • Lower Paleolithic: hand-axes and cleavers (the Acheulian culture), e.g. Attirampakkam (Tamil Nadu) and the Soan valley.
  • Middle Paleolithic: flake tools.
  • Upper Paleolithic: blades and burins; modern humans appear.

Examples:

  • Tools were mostly made of quartzite.
  • Narmada Man, a skull found at Hathnora (Madhya Pradesh), is the only Paleolithic human fossil from India.
  • The Kurnool caves (Andhra Pradesh) show early use of fire.

Remember: Paleolithic people knew fire and stone tools, but not farming, pottery or metals.

4. Mesolithic Age (Middle Stone Age)

The Mesolithic Age (c. 10,000–6000 BCE) began as the Ice Age ended and the climate became warmer. Its hallmark is microliths: very small stone tools fixed onto wood or bone to make arrows, spears and sickles.

Examples:

  • Adamgarh (Madhya Pradesh) and Bagor (Rajasthan) give the earliest evidence of animal domestication in India.
  • Langhnaj (Gujarat) has microliths and human burials.

Bhimbetka rock paintings

The Bhimbetka rock shelters near Bhopal (Madhya Pradesh), discovered by V. S. Wakankar in 1957, have India's richest prehistoric paintings. They show hunting, animals and dancing, painted with natural colours such as red ochre. Bhimbetka is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

5. Neolithic Age (New Stone Age)

The Neolithic Age brought the Neolithic Revolution: the change from gathering food to producing it by farming and herding. People began to live in settled villages.

  • Tools were ground and polished, not just chipped.
  • Pottery was made, to store and cook food.

Important Neolithic sites

  • Mehrgarh (Baluchistan, Pakistan; c. 7000 BCE): the earliest farming in the subcontinent, of wheat and barley.
  • Burzahom (Kashmir): pit-dwellings, and dogs buried with their masters.
  • Koldihwa (Belan valley, Uttar Pradesh): early rice cultivation.
  • Chirand (Bihar): many bone tools.
  • Maski, Brahmagiri, Hallur (Karnataka): the South Indian Neolithic.
  • Daojali Hading (Assam): the North-East Neolithic.

6. Chalcolithic Age (Copper-Stone Age)

The Chalcolithic Age came after the Neolithic. Copper, the first metal people used, was used alongside stone tools, mostly by rural farming communities.

Examples:

  • Ahar-Banas culture (south-eastern Rajasthan).
  • Jorwe culture (Maharashtra): Inamgaon and Daimabad. Daimabad is famous for its bronze chariot.
  • The Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP) culture, linked with the Copper Hoards of the Ganga–Yamuna region.

Stone Age → Chalcolithic (copper) → Bronze Age (Harappan) → Iron Age (Later Vedic)

7. Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrect understanding
Paleolithic people were farmers.They were hunter-gatherers; farming began in the Neolithic Age.
Microliths belong to the Old Stone Age.Microliths are the hallmark of the Mesolithic (Middle Stone Age).
Bhimbetka's paintings are like Ajanta's.Bhimbetka is prehistoric rock art; Ajanta is much later, from historic times.
Iron was the first metal people used.Copper came first (Chalcolithic); iron came much later.
Prehistory is known from old books.There was no writing; prehistory is known only from archaeology.
