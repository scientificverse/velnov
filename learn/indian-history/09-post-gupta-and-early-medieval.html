Topic overview

Post-Gupta and Early Medieval India – regional kingdoms (c. 550–1200 CE)

In this overview: Harshavardhana · Kingdoms of the Deccan and the South · The struggle for Kannauj · The Rajputs · Arab and Turkish invasions · Religion and culture · Common misconceptions · Quick revision · Test your understanding

1. Harshavardhana

Harsha (606–647 CE) of the Pushyabhuti dynasty ruled north India from Kannauj.

  • His court poet Banabhatta wrote the Harshacharita (his biography) and Kadambari.
  • The Chinese pilgrim Hiuen Tsang visited, studied at Nalanda, and wrote the Si-yu-ki.
  • Harsha himself wrote three Sanskrit plays: Ratnavali, Nagananda and Priyadarshika.
  • Every five years he held a great assembly at Prayag and gave away his wealth.

Example: Harsha's march south was stopped on the Narmada by the Chalukya king Pulakeshin II, as recorded in the Aihole inscription by the poet Ravikirti.

2. Kingdoms of the Deccan and the South

A. Chalukyas of Badami

Their greatest king was Pulakeshin II. They built temples at Badami, Aihole and Pattadakal, in the Vesara style, which mixes northern and southern features.

B. Pallavas of Kanchi

  • Mahendravarman I began rock-cut temples and wrote the play Mattavilasa Prahasana.
  • Narasimhavarman I (Mamalla) defeated and killed Pulakeshin II and captured Badami.
  • They built the Shore Temple and the rathas (chariot temples) at Mahabalipuram, and the Kailasanatha Temple at Kanchi.

C. Rashtrakutas

  • Dantidurga founded the dynasty, replacing the Chalukyas.
  • Krishna I built the Kailasa Temple at Ellora, carved from a single rock, from the top down.
  • Amoghavarsha wrote the Kavirajamarga, the earliest Kannada work on poetics. Their capital was Manyakheta.

D. The Imperial Cholas

The Cholas (9th–13th centuries) were the greatest power of the South.

  • Rajaraja I built the Brihadeeswara Temple at Thanjavur.
  • Rajendra I reached the Ganga (taking the title Gangaikonda Chola) and sent a navy against the Srivijaya empire of South-East Asia. The Bay of Bengal was called the Chola Lake.
  • Village assemblies (the Ur and the Sabha) ran local affairs; the Uttaramerur inscriptions describe how their members were chosen.
  • Chola bronzes, above all the dancing Nataraja, are world famous.

3. The struggle for Kannauj

After Harsha, Kannauj became the prize in the Tripartite Struggle among three powers:

  • the Palas of Bengal,
  • the Gurjara-Pratiharas of the north (who finally held it),
  • the Rashtrakutas of the Deccan.

Examples:

  • The Palas were great patrons of Buddhism. Dharmapala founded Vikramashila university.
  • The long struggle left north India divided and weak against later invaders.

4. The Rajputs

From about the 8th century, warrior clans called Rajputs (from rajaputra, “son of a king”) ruled much of north and west India.

Examples:

  • Chandelas: the Khajuraho temples.
  • Solankis of Gujarat: the Sun Temple at Modhera and the Rani ki Vav stepwell.
  • Gurjara-Pratiharas: held back the Arabs.

Remember: The Rajputs were brave, but their constant rivalries kept them from uniting against the Turks.

5. Arab and Turkish invasions

  • 712 CE: Muhammad bin Qasim conquered Sindh, the first Arab foothold.
  • Mahmud of Ghazni raided India about 17 times, most famously plundering the Somnath Temple (1025 CE). The scholar Al-Biruni came with him and wrote Kitab-ul-Hind.
  • 1191: Prithviraj Chauhan defeated Muhammad Ghori at the First Battle of Tarain.
  • 1192: Ghori won the Second Battle of Tarain, opening the way for the Delhi Sultanate (1206).

6. Religion and culture

  • Adi Shankaracharya taught Advaita (non-dualism) and set up four mathas.
  • Ramanuja taught Vishishtadvaita, and Madhva taught Dvaita.
  • The Tamil Alvars (devotees of Vishnu) and Nayanars (devotees of Shiva) spread Bhakti, devotion to God.
  • Jayadeva wrote the Gita Govinda, and Kalhana wrote the Rajatarangini, a history of Kashmir.
  • Buddhism declined in India, while Hindu temple-building flourished.

Example: Other great temples: the Hoysala temples at Belur and Halebidu, and the Sun Temple at Konark, built by Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Gangas.

Q2. hard

The dynasty to which Harshavardhana belonged was the:

  1. A Pushyabhuti (Vardhana) dynasty
  2. B Gupta dynasty
  3. C Maurya dynasty
  4. D Chola dynasty
