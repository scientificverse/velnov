Topic overview

Nutrition in Animals – from eating to digesting

In this overview: The five steps of nutrition · The human digestive system · The mouth · The oesophagus and stomach · Liver and pancreas · The small intestine · The large intestine · How other animals feed · Common misconceptions · Quick revision · Test your understanding

1. The five steps of nutrition

Animals are heterotrophs. Their nutrition happens in five steps:

  • Ingestion: taking food into the body.
  • Digestion: breaking complex food into simple, soluble substances.
  • Absorption: passing digested food into the blood.
  • Assimilation: cells using the absorbed food for energy, growth and repair.
  • Egestion: removing undigested food as faeces.

Ingestion → Digestion → Absorption → Assimilation → Egestion

2. The human digestive system

The alimentary canal is a long tube from mouth to anus. Digestive glands (salivary glands, liver and pancreas) pour juices into it.

Mouth → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine → Rectum → Anus

3. The mouth

Digestion begins in the mouth. Teeth break food into small pieces, and saliva mixes with it.

Saliva contains the enzyme salivary amylase, which turns starch into sugar.

Example: Chew a piece of chapati for a long time and it begins to taste sweet.

Teeth

  • Incisors (8): cut and bite.
  • Canines (4): tear and pierce.
  • Premolars (8) and molars (12): crush and grind.

An adult has 32 teeth; a child has 20 milk teeth.

Remember: Bacteria turn leftover sugar into acid, which attacks the enamel and causes tooth decay. Brush after meals.

The tongue

The tongue mixes food with saliva, rolls it into a ball, helps us swallow, and has taste buds.

4. The oesophagus and stomach

The oesophagus (food pipe) carries food to the stomach by peristalsis: wave-like squeezing of its muscles.

The stomach produces:

  • Hydrochloric acid, which kills germs and helps the enzymes work,
  • digestive juices that start breaking down proteins (the enzyme pepsin),
  • mucus, which protects the stomach wall from its own acid.

Example: The epiglottis, a small flap, covers the windpipe when you swallow, so food doesn't go down the wrong way.

5. Liver and pancreas

  • The liver, the largest gland in the body, makes bile, which is stored in the gall bladder. Bile breaks fat into tiny droplets so it can be digested.
  • The pancreas makes pancreatic juice, which digests carbohydrates, proteins and fats.

6. The small intestine

Most digestion and absorption happen in the small intestine, which is about 7.5 metres long.

Its inner wall has millions of finger-like villi. They give a huge surface area for absorbing digested food into the blood.

What food is broken into

  • Carbohydrates → glucose.
  • Proteins → amino acids.
  • Fats → fatty acids and glycerol.

Enzymes are the substances that speed up these breakdowns, e.g. amylase (starch), pepsin (protein) and lipase (fat).

7. The large intestine

The large intestine absorbs water and some salts from undigested food. The waste is stored in the rectum and passed out through the anus (egestion).

Examples:

  • Too little roughage and water causes constipation.
  • Diarrhoea drains water and salts from the body. ORS, a pinch of salt and a spoon of sugar in clean water, replaces them.

8. How other animals feed

A. Amoeba

Amoeba pushes out finger-like pseudopodia around its food and traps it in a food vacuole, where the food is digested.

B. Grass-eaters (ruminants)

Ruminants such as cows swallow grass quickly and store it in the rumen. Later they bring it back up and chew it again: chewing the cud.

Bacteria in their stomach digest cellulose, which humans cannot digest.

Example: Horses and rabbits have a large caecum, where bacteria digest cellulose.

C. Other feeding methods

  • Mosquito: sucks blood through a needle-like mouthpart.
  • Butterfly: sips nectar through a long, coiled tube.
  • Snake: swallows its prey whole.
  • Starfish: pushes its stomach out of its mouth onto its prey, and digests it outside its body.

9. Common misconceptions

Egestion and excretion are the same.Egestion removes undigested food; excretion removes wastes like urea made by the body.
Bile is an enzyme.Bile has no enzymes; it breaks fat into droplets so enzymes can work on it.

Easy questions (2)

Q1. easy

The breaking down of complex food into simpler absorbable substances is called:

  1. A Digestion
  2. B Ingestion
  3. C Egestion
  4. D Absorption

Answer: A — Digestion

Digestion converts large insoluble food molecules into small soluble ones that can pass into the blood. It involves both mechanical and chemical action.

Digestion

Q2. easy

The taking in of food into the body is called:

  1. A Ingestion
  2. B Digestion
  3. C Absorption
  4. D Assimilation

Answer: A — Ingestion

Ingestion is the first step of nutrition - putting food into the mouth. It is followed by digestion, absorption, assimilation and egestion.

Ingestion

Medium questions (30)

Q3. medium

The five steps of nutrition in animals, in order, are:

  1. A Ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation, egestion
  2. B Digestion, ingestion, egestion, absorption, assimilation
  3. C Absorption, ingestion, digestion, egestion, assimilation
  4. D Egestion, ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation

Answer: A — Ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation, egestion

Food is taken in, broken down, absorbed into the blood, used by the cells (assimilation), and the undigested part is finally removed as faeces (egestion).

Steps of nutrition

  1. A Absorption
  2. B Ingestion
  3. C Egestion
  4. D Digestion

Absorption takes place mainly in the small intestine, where the digested nutrients pass through the intestinal wall into the blood stream.

Absorption

Q6. medium

The removal of undigested food from the body is called:

  1. A Egestion
  2. B Excretion

Egestion removes the undigested residue as faeces through the anus. It is different from excretion, which removes metabolic wastes such as urea.

Egestion

  3. C The teeth

Answer: A — The epiglottis, a flap that covers the windpipe

As you swallow, the epiglottis folds down over the opening of the windpipe. If food slips past it, we choke and cough to expel it.

Q60. hard

The small intestine of a herbivore like a cow is much longer than that of a tiger because:

  1. A Plant food is harder to digest and needs a longer time in the gut
  2. B Cows eat more often
  3. C Tigers do not digest food
  4. D Cows are bigger animals

Answer: A — Plant food is harder to digest and needs a longer time in the gut

Cellulose-rich grass breaks down slowly, so herbivores need a long gut and helpful bacteria. Meat is digested quickly, so carnivores manage with a much shorter intestine.
