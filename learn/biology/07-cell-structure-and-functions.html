Easy questions (2)

Q1. easy

The basic structural and functional unit of all living organisms is the:

  1. A Cell
  2. B Tissue
  3. C Organ
  4. D Organ system

Answer: A — Cell

Every living thing, from a bacterium to a blue whale, is made of cells. The cell is the smallest unit that shows all the characteristics of life.

Cell

Q2. easy

Who discovered the cell in 1665?

  1. A Robert Hooke
  2. B Robert Brown
  3. C Leeuwenhoek
  4. D Charles Darwin

Answer: A — Robert Hooke

Robert Hooke examined a thin slice of cork under his microscope and saw tiny box-like compartments. He named them 'cells' because they reminded him of the small rooms in a monastery.

Robert HookeDiscovery of cell

Medium questions (31)

Q3. medium

Robert Hooke observed cells in a thin slice of:

  1. A Cork
  2. B Onion
  3. C Leaf
  4. D Blood

Answer: A — Cork

Cork is dead plant tissue from tree bark. What Hooke actually saw were the empty walls of dead cells - he never saw a living cell.

CorkRobert Hooke

Q4. medium

Who first observed LIVING cells (tiny moving organisms) under a microscope?

  1. A Anton van Leeuwenhoek
  2. B Robert Hooke
  3. C Robert Brown
  4. D Purkinje

Answer: A — Anton van Leeuwenhoek

Leeuwenhoek made powerful simple microscopes and in 1674 observed living single-celled organisms in pond water, which he called 'animalcules'.

LeeuwenhoekLiving cells

Q6. medium

Organisms made of only ONE cell are called:

  1. A Unicellular
  2. B Multicellular
  3. C Acellular
  4. D Bicellular

Answer: A — Unicellular

Amoeba, Paramecium, bacteria and yeast are unicellular. That single cell performs every life process by itself.

Unicellular

Q7. medium

Organisms made of MANY cells are called:

  1. A Multicellular
  2. B Unicellular
  3. C Single-celled
  4. D Prokaryotic

Answer: A — Multicellular

Plants, animals and humans are multicellular. Their cells are specialised for different jobs and work together as tissues, organs and organ systems.

Multicellular

Q9. medium

Which is generally the LONGEST cell in the human body?

  1. A The nerve cell (neuron)
  2. B The red blood cell
  3. C The muscle cell
  4. D The skin cell

Answer: A — The nerve cell (neuron)

A single neuron can be a metre long, running from the spinal cord to the toe. Its length lets it carry signals over long distances quickly.

NeuronLongest cell

Q11. medium

The three basic parts found in ALL cells are:

  1. A Cell membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus (or nuclear material)
  2. B Cell wall, chloroplast and vacuole
  3. C Nucleus, chloroplast and cell wall
  4. D Mitochondria, vacuole and cell wall

Answer: A — Cell membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus (or nuclear material)

Every cell is bounded by a cell membrane, filled with cytoplasm, and contains genetic material. Cell walls and chloroplasts are found only in plant cells.

Cell parts

Q13. medium

The cell membrane is described as SEMI-PERMEABLE because it:

  1. A Allows only certain substances to pass in and out
  2. B Allows everything to pass
  3. C Allows nothing to pass
  4. D Is made of cellulose

Answer: A — Allows only certain substances to pass in and out

This selective control keeps useful substances in and harmful ones out, and lets the cell regulate its internal environment.

Semi-permeable membrane

Q15. medium

The jelly-like substance between the cell membrane and the nucleus is:

  1. A Cytoplasm
  2. B Nucleoplasm
  3. C Chlorophyll
  4. D Vacuole sap

Answer: A — Cytoplasm

Cytoplasm holds all the cell organelles and is the site of many chemical reactions that keep the cell alive.

Cytoplasm

Q17. medium

The nucleus is often called the 'brain' or control centre of the cell because it:

  1. A Controls all the activities of the cell and carries the hereditary material
  2. B Makes food
  3. C Stores water
  4. D Produces energy

Answer: A — Controls all the activities of the cell and carries the hereditary material

The nucleus directs growth, repair and reproduction, and contains chromosomes carrying the genetic information passed to the next generation.

NucleusControl centre

Q19. medium

The units of inheritance that pass characters from parents to offspring are called:

  1. A Genes
  2. B Cells
  3. C Tissues
  4. D Vacuoles

Answer: A — Genes

Genes are segments of DNA on chromosomes. They determine features such as eye colour, height and blood group, and are passed on during reproduction.

GenesHeredity

Q21. medium

The dense round body inside the nucleus is the:

  1. A Nucleolus
  2. B Vacuole
  3. C Chloroplast
  4. D Ribosome

Answer: A — Nucleolus

The nucleolus is a small spherical structure within the nucleus. It is involved in making ribosomes, which in turn make proteins.

Nucleolus

Q23. medium

Cells WITHOUT a well-organised nucleus, such as bacteria, are called:

  1. A Prokaryotic cells
  2. B Eukaryotic cells
  3. C Plant cells
  4. D Animal cells

Answer: A — Prokaryotic cells

In a prokaryotic cell, the genetic material is not enclosed in a nuclear membrane and floats in the cytoplasm. Such cells also lack most membrane-bound organelles.

Prokaryotic cellsBacteria

Q25. medium

Structures in the cytoplasm that carry out specific functions are called:

  1. A Organelles
  2. B Tissues
  3. C Organs
  4. D Chromosomes

Answer: A — Organelles

Just as organs perform tasks in the body, organelles such as mitochondria, ribosomes, plastids and vacuoles perform particular jobs inside the cell.

Organelles

Q27. medium

Green plastids that contain chlorophyll and carry out photosynthesis are called:

  1. A Chloroplasts
  2. B Chromoplasts
  3. C Leucoplasts
  4. D Mitochondria

Answer: A — Chloroplasts

Chloroplasts are found in plant cells exposed to light, mainly in leaves. They trap sunlight and make glucose - the process on which all food chains depend.

ChloroplastPhotosynthesis

Q29. medium

The large fluid-filled space in a plant cell is called the:

  1. A Vacuole
  2. B Nucleus
  3. C Mitochondrion
  4. D Ribosome

Answer: A — Vacuole

A mature plant cell usually has one big central vacuole filled with cell sap. It stores water and dissolved substances and keeps the cell firm.

VacuoleCell sap

Q31. medium

Animal cells have vacuoles that are:

  1. A Small and numerous, if present at all
  2. B Very large and single
  3. C Always absent
  4. D Bigger than in plants

Answer: A — Small and numerous, if present at all

Animal cells have several tiny vacuoles or none. The huge single vacuole is a distinguishing feature of mature plant cells.

VacuoleAnimal cells

Q33. medium

Plant cells usually have a fixed, rectangular shape because of the:

  1. A Rigid cell wall
  2. B Nucleus
  3. C Vacuole only
  4. D Chloroplast

Answer: A — Rigid cell wall

The tough cellulose wall holds the cell in a definite shape. Animal cells, lacking a wall, are usually rounded and irregular.

Cell wallShape

Q35. medium

Amoeba has no fixed shape because it:

  1. A Constantly changes shape by extending pseudopodia
  2. B Has a thick cell wall
  3. C Has no cell membrane
  4. D Is not a cell

Answer: A — Constantly changes shape by extending pseudopodia

Amoeba pushes out finger-like pseudopodia to move and to capture food, so its outline is always changing. It has a flexible membrane but no cell wall.

AmoebaPseudopodia

Q37. medium

The correct order of organisation in a multicellular organism is:

  1. A Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ system → Organism
  2. B Tissue → Cell → Organ → Organism
  3. C Organ → Tissue → Cell → Organism
  4. D Organism → Organ → Cell → Tissue

Answer: A — Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ system → Organism

Each level is built from the one before. This hierarchy allows division of labour, with different parts specialising in different jobs.

Levels of organisation

Q39. medium

While preparing an onion peel slide, a stain such as iodine or safranin is used to:

  1. A Make the cell parts clearly visible under the microscope
  2. B Kill the cells
  3. C Add colour for beauty
  4. D Keep the slide wet

Answer: A — Make the cell parts clearly visible under the microscope

Cell structures are transparent and hard to see. Stains are absorbed differently by different parts, so the nucleus and cell wall stand out clearly.

StainingMicroscopy

Q41. medium

Human cheek cells observed under a microscope appear:

  1. A Rounded or irregular, with no cell wall and a clear nucleus
  2. B Rectangular with thick walls
  3. C Green
  4. D Long and thread-like

Answer: A — Rounded or irregular, with no cell wall and a clear nucleus

Cheek cells are typical animal cells: a flexible membrane rather than a wall, an irregular flattened shape, and a distinct nucleus when stained.

Cheek cellsAnimal cell

Q43. medium

Which scientists are credited with proposing the Cell Theory?

  1. A Schleiden and Schwann
  2. B Hooke and Brown
  3. C Darwin and Mendel
  4. D Newton and Einstein

Answer: A — Schleiden and Schwann

The botanist Matthias Schleiden and the zoologist Theodor Schwann proposed in 1838-39 that plants and animals alike are composed of cells.

Cell theorySchleidenSchwann

Q45. medium

Red blood cells are unusual because a mature human red blood cell:

  1. A Has no nucleus, leaving more room to carry oxygen
  2. B Has two nuclei
  3. C Has a cell wall
  4. D Contains chloroplasts

Answer: A — Has no nucleus, leaving more room to carry oxygen

Losing the nucleus lets the cell pack in more haemoglobin and take on a flexible biconcave shape, but it also means the cell lives only about 120 days.

Red blood cellsNucleus

Q47. medium

The endoplasmic reticulum is a network of tubes that:

  1. A Transports materials within the cell
  2. B Produces energy
  3. C Digests food
  4. D Stores genetic material

Answer: A — Transports materials within the cell

The endoplasmic reticulum acts as an internal transport and manufacturing system. Rough ER studded with ribosomes makes proteins; smooth ER makes fats.

Endoplasmic reticulum

Q49. medium

Which organelle packages and dispatches materials made by the cell?

  1. A The Golgi apparatus
  2. B The nucleus
  3. C The vacuole
  4. D The ribosome

Answer: A — The Golgi apparatus

The Golgi apparatus modifies, sorts and packs proteins and other substances into vesicles for storage inside the cell or export outside it.

Golgi apparatus

Q51. medium

The tail of a sperm cell is an adaptation for:

  1. A Swimming towards the egg
  2. B Carrying oxygen
  3. C Digesting food
  4. D Absorbing water

Answer: A — Swimming towards the egg

The whip-like tail propels the sperm through fluid to reach the egg. The head carries the genetic material and enzymes to penetrate the egg's surface.

Sperm cellSpecialisation

Q53. medium

Which of these correctly compares a plant cell and an animal cell?

  1. A Plant cells have a cell wall, chloroplasts and a large vacuole; animal cells do not
  2. B Animal cells have cell walls
  3. C Plant cells have no nucleus
  4. D They are identical

Answer: A — Plant cells have a cell wall, chloroplasts and a large vacuole; animal cells do not

Both have a membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria and ribosomes, but the wall, plastids and large central vacuole are unique to the plant cell.

ComparisonPlant vs animal cell

Q55. medium

A cell placed in pure water swells because water enters by:

  1. A Osmosis
  2. B Respiration
  3. C Digestion
  4. D Photosynthesis

Answer: A — Osmosis

Water moves through the semi-permeable membrane from the more dilute outside to the more concentrated cell contents. Plant cells swell only until the cell wall stops them.

Osmosis

Q57. medium

Which overall statement about cells is CORRECT?

  1. A All living things are made of cells, each with a membrane, cytoplasm and genetic material, and cells vary in size, shape and function
  2. B Only plants are made of cells
  3. C All cells are identical
  4. D Cells are non-living

Answer: A — All living things are made of cells, each with a membrane, cytoplasm and genetic material, and cells vary in size, shape and function

This sums up the topic: the cell is the fundamental unit of life; organelles divide the work inside it; and specialised cells form tissues, organs and whole organisms.

Summary

Q58. medium

Why are most cells microscopic in size rather than very large?

  1. A A small size gives a large surface area relative to volume, so materials move in and out efficiently
  2. B Large cells are impossible
  3. C Small cells look nicer
  4. D Size makes no difference

Answer: A — A small size gives a large surface area relative to volume, so materials move in and out efficiently

As a cell grows, its volume increases faster than its surface. A small cell has plenty of membrane relative to its contents, so oxygen, food and wastes cross quickly enough to keep it alive.

Cell sizeSurface area to volume

Q60. medium

Which structure controls what enters and leaves BOTH plant and animal cells?

  1. A The cell membrane
  2. B The cell wall
  3. C The nucleolus
  4. D The vacuole

Answer: A — The cell membrane

The selectively permeable cell membrane surrounds every cell and regulates the passage of substances. The cell wall, present only in plants, lies outside it and is freely permeable.

Cell membraneSelective permeability

Who discovered the NUCLEUS of the cell in 1831?

  1. A Robert Brown

Answer: A — Robert Brown

Robert Brown identified the dense round body present in cells and named it the nucleus. It was later found to control all the cell's activities.

  1. A The egg of an ostrich
  2. B A human nerve cell
  3. C A red blood cell

Answer: A — The egg of an ostrich

An ostrich egg is a single cell and is the largest known. Among human cells, the female egg cell is the largest and can just be seen without a microscope.

Largest cellOstrich egg

Q10. hard

Which is the SMALLEST cell?

  1. A A bacterial cell (Mycoplasma)
  2. B A human egg
  3. C A plant cell
  4. D A nerve cell

Answer: A — A bacterial cell (Mycoplasma)

Mycoplasma, at about 0.1 micrometre, is the smallest known cell. Bacteria in general are far smaller than plant or animal cells.

Smallest cellBacteria

Q12. hard

The thin outer covering present in ALL cells is the:

  1. A Cell membrane (plasma membrane)
  2. B Cell wall
  3. C Nuclear membrane
  4. D Cytoplasm

Answer: A — Cell membrane (plasma membrane)

The cell membrane gives the cell its shape and controls which substances enter and leave. Because it is selective, it is called semi-permeable.

Cell membrane

Q14. hard

The rigid outer layer found ONLY in plant cells is the:

  1. A Cell wall, made of cellulose
  2. B Cell membrane
  3. C Nuclear membrane
  4. D Vacuole

Answer: A — Cell wall, made of cellulose

The cellulose cell wall lies outside the cell membrane, giving the plant cell a fixed rectangular shape, strength and protection. Animal cells have no cell wall.

Cell wallCellulose

Q16. hard

The living substance of the cell, including the cytoplasm and nucleus, is called:

  1. A Protoplasm
  2. B Cellulose
  3. C Chlorophyll
  4. D Plasma

Answer: A — Protoplasm

Protoplasm was described by Purkinje as 'the living substance of the cell'. It comprises the cytoplasm together with the nucleus.

Protoplasm

Q18. hard

The thread-like structures inside the nucleus that carry genes are called:

  1. A Chromosomes
  2. B Ribosomes
  3. C Vacuoles
  4. D Plastids

Answer: A — Chromosomes

Chromosomes are made of DNA and protein and carry the genes that determine inherited characteristics. Human body cells contain 46 chromosomes (23 pairs).

ChromosomesGenes

Q20. hard

How many chromosomes are present in a normal human body cell?

  1. A 46 (23 pairs)
  2. B 23
  3. C 48
  4. D 92

Answer: A — 46 (23 pairs)

Each body cell has 46 chromosomes - 23 inherited from each parent. Egg and sperm cells contain only 23 each, so the number stays constant across generations.

ChromosomesHuman

Q22. hard

Cells that have a well-defined nucleus surrounded by a nuclear membrane are called:

  1. A Eukaryotic cells
  2. B Prokaryotic cells
  3. C Bacterial cells
  4. D Acellular

Answer: A — Eukaryotic cells

Plants, animals, fungi and protists have eukaryotic cells. Bacteria and blue-green algae, whose genetic material lies free in the cytoplasm, are prokaryotic.

Eukaryotic cells

Q24. hard

The organelle known as the 'powerhouse of the cell' is the:

  1. A Mitochondrion
  2. B Nucleus
  3. C Ribosome
  4. D Vacuole

Answer: A — Mitochondrion

Mitochondria carry out respiration, releasing energy from food and storing it as ATP. Cells that need a lot of energy, such as muscle cells, have many mitochondria.

MitochondriaEnergy

Q26. hard

The organelles where proteins are made are the:

  1. A Ribosomes
  2. B Mitochondria
  3. C Vacuoles
  4. D Plastids

Answer: A — Ribosomes

Ribosomes are tiny granular structures, free in the cytoplasm or attached to the endoplasmic reticulum, that assemble amino acids into proteins.

RibosomesProtein synthesis

Q28. hard

Plastids are found in:

  1. A Plant cells only
  2. B Animal cells only
  3. C Both equally
  4. D Bacteria only

Answer: A — Plant cells only

Plastids - chloroplasts, coloured chromoplasts and colourless food-storing leucoplasts - are unique to plant cells. Animal cells have none.

PlastidsPlant cells

Q30. hard

The large central vacuole in a plant cell helps by:

  1. A Storing water and salts, and keeping the cell firm (turgid)
  2. B Making food
  3. C Producing energy
  4. D Making proteins

Answer: A — Storing water and salts, and keeping the cell firm (turgid)

Water pressure in the vacuole pushes the cytoplasm against the cell wall, giving the cell rigidity. When plants lose water, the vacuoles shrink and the plant wilts.

VacuoleTurgidity

Q32. hard

Which of these is found in a PLANT cell but NOT in an animal cell?

  1. A Cell wall, chloroplasts and a large central vacuole
  2. B Nucleus
  3. C Cell membrane
  4. D Cytoplasm

Answer: A — Cell wall, chloroplasts and a large central vacuole

These three features distinguish plant cells. Both cell types have a membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria and ribosomes.

Plant vs animal cell

Q34. hard

Which of these best describes the shape of cells?

  1. A Cells vary in shape according to their function - some are round, some long, some spindle-shaped
  2. B All cells are round
  3. C All cells are square
  4. D Shape is always the same

Answer: A — Cells vary in shape according to their function - some are round, some long, some spindle-shaped

Nerve cells are long and branched to carry signals, red blood cells are disc-shaped to squeeze through capillaries, and muscle cells are spindle-shaped for contraction.

Cell shapeFunction

Q36. hard

A group of similar cells performing a particular function is called a:

  1. A Tissue
  2. B Organ
  3. C Organ system
  4. D Organism

Answer: A — Tissue

Muscle tissue, nerve tissue and xylem are examples. Tissues combine to form organs, organs form organ systems, and systems together form the organism.

Tissue

Q38. hard

Which instrument is used to observe cells?

  1. A A microscope
  2. B A telescope
  3. C A stethoscope
  4. D A barometer

Answer: A — A microscope

Cells are far too small for the naked eye. A compound microscope magnifies them hundreds of times, and an electron microscope reveals even the organelles inside.

Microscope

Q42. hard

The Cell Theory states that:

  1. A All living things are made of cells, and all cells arise from pre-existing cells
  2. B Cells arise from non-living matter
  3. C Only plants have cells
  4. D Cells are not important

Answer: A — All living things are made of cells, and all cells arise from pre-existing cells

Schleiden and Schwann proposed that all organisms are made of cells, and Virchow added that every cell comes from a pre-existing cell by division.

Cell theory

Q44. hard

Rudolf Virchow added to the cell theory the idea that:

  1. A New cells arise only from the division of existing cells
  2. B Cells come from air
  3. C Cells never divide
  4. D Cells are non-living

Answer: A — New cells arise only from the division of existing cells

Virchow's phrase 'omnis cellula e cellula' - every cell from a cell - completed the theory and ruled out the idea of life arising spontaneously.

VirchowCell division

Q46. hard

Cells that carry out specialised functions in a multicellular organism show:

  1. A Division of labour - each type of cell does a particular job
  2. B No difference from one another
  3. C The same shape always
  4. D Independent living

Answer: A — Division of labour - each type of cell does a particular job

Specialisation makes the organism efficient: nerve cells carry signals, muscle cells contract, red blood cells carry oxygen and root hair cells absorb water.

Q48. hard

  1. A Contain digestive enzymes that break down worn-out parts and can destroy the cell itself
  2. B Make proteins
  3. C Store water
  4. D Carry out photosynthesis

Q50. hard

Cells divide mainly for:

  1. A Growth, repair of damaged tissue, and reproduction
  2. B Only for fun
  3. C To reduce their number

Cell division adds cells so the organism grows, replaces cells lost through wear and injury, and produces the gametes needed for reproduction.

Cell division

Q59. hard
