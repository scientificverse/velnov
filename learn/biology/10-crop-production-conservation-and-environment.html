Easy questions (2)

Q1. easy

When plants of the same kind are grown on a large scale, they are called a:

  1. A Crop
  2. B Forest
  3. C Weed
  4. D Herb

Answer: A — Crop

A crop is a large number of plants of one kind grown and cultivated by people, such as wheat, rice or cotton grown in a field.

Crop

Q2. easy

Crops grown in the RAINY season (June to September) are called:

  1. A Kharif crops
  2. B Rabi crops
  3. C Zaid crops
  4. D Cash crops

Answer: A — Kharif crops

Kharif crops such as paddy, maize, cotton and groundnut are sown at the beginning of the monsoon and harvested in autumn. They need plenty of water.

Kharif crops

Medium questions (30)

Q3. medium

Crops grown in the WINTER season (October to March) are called:

  1. A Rabi crops
  2. B Kharif crops
  3. C Zaid crops
  4. D Weeds

Answer: A — Rabi crops

Rabi crops such as wheat, gram, peas and mustard are sown in winter and harvested in spring. They need less water than kharif crops.

Rabi crops

Q4. medium

The loosening and turning of the soil before sowing is called:

  1. A Ploughing (tilling)
  2. B Sowing
  3. C Harvesting
  4. D Threshing

Answer: A — Ploughing (tilling)

Ploughing loosens the soil so that roots can breathe and penetrate easily, and it brings nutrient-rich soil to the top. It is done with a plough or a tractor-drawn cultivator.

PloughingPreparation of soil

Q6. medium

The placing of seeds in the soil is called:

  1. A Sowing
  2. B Ploughing
  3. C Harvesting
  4. D Winnowing

Answer: A — Sowing

Seeds may be sown by hand or with a seed drill. Good-quality, healthy seeds sown at the correct depth and spacing give a better crop.

Sowing

Q8. medium

Substances added to the soil to supply nutrients to plants are called:

  1. A Manures and fertilisers
  2. B Pesticides
  3. C Weedicides
  4. D Water

Answer: A — Manures and fertilisers

Manures and fertilisers replace the nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium that crops remove from the soil, keeping it fertile for the next crop.

ManureFertilisers

Q10. medium

Which of these is an ADVANTAGE of manure over chemical fertilisers?

  1. A Manure improves soil texture and does not harm the environment
  2. B Manure acts faster
  3. C Manure has no nutrients
  4. D Manure pollutes water

Answer: A — Manure improves soil texture and does not harm the environment

Manure adds humus that improves soil structure and its ability to hold water and air, and it is eco-friendly. Chemical fertilisers act faster but can damage soil and water if overused.

ManureAdvantages

Q12. medium

Which modern irrigation method delivers water DROP by DROP to the roots, saving water?

  1. A Drip irrigation
  2. B Flood irrigation
  3. C Furrow irrigation
  4. D Basin irrigation

Answer: A — Drip irrigation

Drip irrigation uses narrow pipes with tiny openings to drip water directly at the base of each plant. Very little water is wasted, so it is ideal for dry regions.

Drip irrigation

Q14. medium

Unwanted plants that grow along with a crop are called:

  1. A Weeds
  2. B Seedlings
  3. C Fruits
  4. D Manure

Answer: A — Weeds

Weeds compete with the crop for water, nutrients, light and space, reducing the yield. Their removal is called weeding.

Weeds

Q16. medium

The cutting and gathering of a mature crop is called:

  1. A Harvesting
  2. B Sowing
  3. C Ploughing
  4. D Irrigation

Answer: A — Harvesting

Harvesting is done by hand with a sickle or by a machine called a harvester. It marks the end of the crop's growth in the field.

Harvesting

Q18. medium

A machine that both harvests and threshes the crop is called a:

  1. A Combine (combine harvester)
  2. B Seed drill
  3. C Plough
  4. D Sprinkler

Answer: A — Combine (combine harvester)

The combine harvester cuts, threshes and cleans the grain in a single operation, saving a great deal of time and labour on large farms.

Combine harvester

Q20. medium

The rearing of animals on a large scale for food and other products is called:

  1. A Animal husbandry
  2. B Agriculture
  3. C Horticulture
  4. D Sericulture only

Answer: A — Animal husbandry

Animal husbandry includes rearing cattle, buffaloes, goats, poultry and fish for milk, meat, eggs and other products. It complements crop farming.

Animal husbandry

Q22. medium

The rearing of silkworms to obtain silk is called:

  1. A Sericulture
  2. B Apiculture
  3. C Pisciculture
  4. D Agriculture

Answer: A — Sericulture

Sericulture is silk farming. Silkworms are fed mulberry leaves, and the fine thread from their cocoons is unwound to make silk.

SericultureSilk

Q24. medium

The variety of living organisms found in an area is called its:

  1. A Biodiversity
  2. B Habitat
  3. C Ecosystem only
  4. D Population

Answer: A — Biodiversity

Biodiversity is the total range of plants, animals and microorganisms in a region. High biodiversity keeps ecosystems healthy and stable.

Biodiversity

Q26. medium

Which of these is a HARMFUL effect of deforestation?

  1. A Soil erosion, less rainfall, and loss of wildlife habitat
  2. B More oxygen
  3. C Cleaner air
  4. D More rainfall

Answer: A — Soil erosion, less rainfall, and loss of wildlife habitat

Without tree roots to bind it, soil is washed or blown away. Fewer trees mean less transpiration and rainfall, higher temperatures and homeless wildlife.

DeforestationEffects

Q28. medium

The protection and careful management of natural resources is called:

  1. A Conservation
  2. B Exploitation
  3. C Pollution
  4. D Deforestation

Answer: A — Conservation

Conservation means using resources wisely and protecting them so that they remain available for future generations. Forests, water and wildlife all need conservation.

Conservation

Q30. medium

A species that has completely died out and no longer exists is called:

  1. A Extinct
  2. B Endangered
  3. C Endemic
  4. D Rare

Answer: A — Extinct

The dinosaurs and the dodo are extinct - not a single individual survives anywhere. Extinction is permanent, which is why saving endangered species matters.

Extinct speciesDodo

Q32. medium

A protected area where wild animals live in their natural habitat and hunting is banned is a:

  1. A Wildlife sanctuary
  2. B Zoo
  3. C Botanical garden
  4. D Farm

Answer: A — Wildlife sanctuary

In a wildlife sanctuary, animals are protected in their natural surroundings and killing or capturing them is prohibited. People may enter with permission.

Wildlife sanctuary

Q34. medium

A large area that protects biodiversity and includes national parks, sanctuaries and human settlements is called a:

  1. A Biosphere reserve
  2. B Zoo
  3. C Botanical garden
  4. D Farm

Answer: A — Biosphere reserve

A biosphere reserve conserves the biodiversity of a whole region, allowing traditional human life to continue alongside protected wildlife. Nilgiri and Sundarbans are examples.

Biosphere reserve

Q36. medium

The one-horned rhinoceros is protected mainly in which Indian national park?

  1. A Kaziranga (Assam)
  2. B Gir (Gujarat)
  3. C Ranthambore (Rajasthan)
  4. D Sundarbans

Answer: A — Kaziranga (Assam)

Kaziranga National Park in Assam is famous for the great one-horned rhinoceros. Its protection has brought this endangered animal back from the brink.

KazirangaRhinoceros

Q38. medium

Which of these is a way to conserve WATER?

  1. A Rainwater harvesting and drip irrigation
  2. B Wasting water
  3. C Leaving taps running
  4. D Polluting rivers

Answer: A — Rainwater harvesting and drip irrigation

Collecting rainwater to recharge the ground, using efficient drip irrigation and fixing leaks all help conserve our limited fresh water.

Water conservation

Q40. medium

Reforestation is important because forests:

  1. A Give oxygen, hold soil, bring rain and shelter wildlife
  2. B Only provide wood
  3. C Cause pollution
  4. D Reduce rainfall

Answer: A — Give oxygen, hold soil, bring rain and shelter wildlife

Forests release oxygen, absorb carbon dioxide, prevent soil erosion, help rainfall and provide homes for countless species, so replanting them benefits the whole environment.

ReforestationForests

Q42. medium

Which of these is a RENEWABLE natural resource?

  1. A Forests (if replanted)
  2. B Coal
  3. C Petroleum
  4. D Natural gas

Answer: A — Forests (if replanted)

Forests, water, wind and sunlight are renewable - they can be replaced or are continuously available. Coal, petroleum and minerals are non-renewable.

Renewable resources

Q44. medium

The seasonal movement of animals such as birds from one region to another is called:

  1. A Migration
  2. B Hibernation
  3. C Deforestation
  4. D Conservation

Answer: A — Migration

Migratory birds travel long distances to escape harsh weather or to find food and breeding grounds. Wetlands where they rest need protection.

MigrationBirds

Q46. medium

Growing a legume crop like gram or beans in rotation improves the soil because these plants:

  1. A Host Rhizobium bacteria that fix nitrogen in the soil
  2. B Remove all nutrients
  3. C Dry the soil
  4. D Add salt

Answer: A — Host Rhizobium bacteria that fix nitrogen in the soil

Rhizobium in the root nodules of legumes converts atmospheric nitrogen into compounds the soil can use, so less fertiliser is needed for the next crop.

Crop rotationNitrogen fixation

Q48. medium

Chemicals sprayed to kill insects and pests on crops are called:

  1. A Pesticides
  2. B Fertilisers
  3. C Weedicides
  4. D Manures

Answer: A — Pesticides

Pesticides protect crops from insect attack, but their overuse harms useful insects, pollutes the environment and leaves residues on food, so they must be used carefully.

Pesticides

Q50. medium

Green Revolution in India refers to:

  1. A The large increase in food-grain production using high-yielding seeds and modern methods
  2. B Planting only green plants
  3. C Painting fields green
  4. D A political movement

Answer: A — The large increase in food-grain production using high-yielding seeds and modern methods

From the 1960s, high-yielding varieties, irrigation, fertilisers and machinery greatly raised wheat and rice output, making India self-sufficient in food grains.

Green Revolution

Q52. medium

Vermicomposting is the making of compost using:

  1. A Earthworms
  2. B Fish
  3. C Bees
  4. D Bacteria only

Answer: A — Earthworms

Earthworms feed on organic waste and turn it into nutrient-rich vermicompost. This is an eco-friendly, low-cost way to recycle kitchen and farm waste.

VermicompostingEarthworms

Q54. medium

Which practice helps conserve SOIL and prevent erosion on hilly land?

  1. A Terrace farming and planting trees
  2. B Cutting all trees
  3. C Overgrazing
  4. D Leaving land bare

Answer: A — Terrace farming and planting trees

Cutting steps (terraces) into a hillside slows the flow of water so it cannot wash away the soil, and tree roots bind the soil in place.

Soil conservationTerrace farming

Q56. medium

Which of these is a good way for students to help the environment?

  1. A Planting trees, saving water and electricity, and reducing waste
  2. B Wasting paper
  3. C Littering

Small everyday actions - planting trees, switching off unused lights and taps, avoiding plastic and segregating waste - add up to a real difference for the environment.

EnvironmentConservation

Q58. medium

Which overall statement about crop production and conservation is CORRECT?

  1. A Good farming needs soil preparation, sowing, nutrients, irrigation, protection and harvesting - and natural resources must be conserved for the future
  2. B Crops need no care
  3. C Resources are unlimited
  4. D Conservation is unnecessary

Answer: A — Good farming needs soil preparation, sowing, nutrients, irrigation, protection and harvesting - and natural resources must be conserved for the future

This sums up the topic: raising a crop involves a sequence of steps from ploughing to storage; animal husbandry adds to our food; and forests, water, soil and wildlife must all be conserved so that the Earth can support future generations.

Q59. medium

Growing two or more crops together in the same field at the same time is called:

  1. A Mixed cropping
  2. B Crop rotation
  3. C Monoculture
  4. D Terrace farming

Answer: A — Mixed cropping

Mixed cropping, such as growing wheat with gram, reduces the risk of total crop failure and uses the soil and space more efficiently. If one crop fails, the other may still give a yield.

Mixed cropping

Hard questions (28)

Q5. hard

Why is loosening the soil by ploughing important?

  1. A It allows air into the soil and helps roots grow and absorb nutrients easily
  2. B It removes all water
  3. C It kills the crop
  4. D It has no benefit

Answer: A — It allows air into the soil and helps roots grow and absorb nutrients easily

Loose soil holds air needed by roots and soil organisms, lets roots spread and breathe, and helps water and nutrients move freely.

PloughingSoil aeration

Q7. hard

A tool used for sowing seeds uniformly at the correct depth is the:

  1. A Seed drill
  2. B Sickle
  3. C Plough only
  4. D Winnowing machine

Answer: A — Seed drill

A seed drill, attached to a tractor, sows seeds at even spacing and depth and covers them with soil. This saves seed and ensures uniform germination.

Seed drill

Q9. hard

The natural substance made by decomposing plant and animal waste, added to enrich soil, is called:

  1. A Manure
  2. B Chemical fertiliser
  3. C Pesticide
  4. D Weedicide

Answer: A — Manure

Manure is decomposed organic matter such as cow dung and compost. It improves soil texture and its water-holding capacity, besides adding nutrients.

ManureOrganic

Q11. hard

The supply of water to crops at regular intervals is called:

  1. A Irrigation
  2. B Ploughing
  3. C Harvesting
  4. D Weeding

Answer: A — Irrigation

Irrigation provides the water crops need to grow. Sources include wells, tube wells, ponds, lakes, rivers, dams and canals.

Irrigation

Q13. hard

The sprinkler system of irrigation is especially useful for:

  1. A Sandy and uneven land where water needs to be spread like rain
  2. B Water-logged fields
  3. C Only paddy fields
  4. D Ponds

Answer: A — Sandy and uneven land where water needs to be spread like rain

Sprinklers spray water through rotating nozzles, imitating rainfall. They suit uneven or sandy soils where flooding would waste water or wash away seeds.

Sprinkler irrigation

Q15. hard

Chemicals used to control or kill weeds are called:

  1. A Weedicides (herbicides)
  2. B Pesticides
  3. C Fertilisers
  4. D Fungicides

Answer: A — Weedicides (herbicides)

Weedicides such as 2,4-D are sprayed to kill weeds without harming the crop. Weeds can also be removed by hand, by tools, or by ploughing before sowing.

Weedicide

Q17. hard

The process of separating grain seeds from the harvested stalks is called:

  1. A Threshing
  2. B Winnowing
  3. C Sowing
  4. D Irrigation

Answer: A — Threshing

Threshing beats or rubs the harvested crop to free the grain from the chaff and stalks. A combine harvester both harvests and threshes in one operation.

Threshing

Q19. hard

Grains meant for storage must first be DRIED because:

  1. A Moisture would allow microorganisms and pests to spoil them
  2. B Dry grains taste better only
  3. C Drying adds nutrients
  4. D Wet grains are lighter

Answer: A — Moisture would allow microorganisms and pests to spoil them

Damp grains are quickly attacked by fungi, bacteria and insects. Proper drying in the sun before storage keeps them safe for months.

StorageDrying

Apiculture is bee-keeping. Bees are reared in artificial hives to obtain honey and beeswax, and they also pollinate nearby crops.

ApicultureBee-keeping

Q23. hard

  1. A Pisciculture
  2. B Apiculture
  3. C Sericulture

Pisciculture, or fish farming, raises fish in ponds and tanks. It is an important source of protein and income in many parts of India.

PiscicultureFish farming

Q25. hard

The gradual disappearance of forests due to human activities is called:

  1. A Deforestation
  2. B Afforestation
  3. C Reforestation
  4. D Conservation

Answer: A — Deforestation

Deforestation is the clearing of forests for farming, building, roads and industry. It reduces biodiversity, causes soil erosion and increases carbon dioxide in the air.

Deforestation

Q27. hard

The planting of trees to create new forests is called:

  1. A Afforestation
  2. B Deforestation
  3. C Irrigation
  4. D Weeding

Answer: A — Afforestation

Afforestation restores green cover. Replanting trees where a forest once stood is called reforestation. Both help fight the effects of deforestation.

Afforestation

Q29. hard

A species whose numbers have fallen so low that it may soon become extinct is called:

  1. A An endangered species
  2. B An extinct species
  3. C A common species
  4. D A domestic species

The tiger, one-horned rhinoceros, Asiatic lion and great Indian bustard are endangered. Special protection is needed to save them from extinction.

Endangered species

Q31. hard

Species found ONLY in a particular area and nowhere else are called:

  1. A Endemic species
  2. B Migratory species
  3. C Extinct species
  4. D Common species

Answer: A — Endemic species

Endemic species are native to a single region. The Asiatic lion (Gir forest) and the Nilgiri tahr are endemic to particular parts of India.

Endemic species

  1. A National park

  4. D Reserve forest only

National parks protect entire ecosystems. No private activity such as grazing, farming or cutting wood is allowed. Jim Corbett and Kaziranga are famous Indian national parks.

National park

Project Tiger

  1. A Crop rotation

Q47. hard

The excessive use of chemical fertilisers is harmful because it:

  1. A Damages soil fertility over time and pollutes water bodies
  2. B Improves the soil forever
  3. C Adds no nutrients
  4. D Has no effect

Answer: A — Damages soil fertility over time and pollutes water bodies

Overuse makes the soil acidic or saline and kills useful soil organisms. Rain washes the excess into rivers and lakes, causing algal blooms and killing fish.

Chemical fertilisersPollution

Q49. hard

Why is the overuse of pesticides a concern?

  1. A They kill helpful insects, pollute water and food, and harm human health
  2. B They make crops grow slower only
  3. C They add nutrients
  4. D They have no drawbacks

Answer: A — They kill helpful insects, pollute water and food, and harm human health

Pesticides do not distinguish between pests and helpful insects like bees. Their residues accumulate in the food chain, harming birds, animals and people.

PesticidesEnvironment

  1. A Loss of vegetation cover, soil erosion and desertification

When too many animals graze an area, the grass cannot recover, the bare soil erodes, and fertile land can gradually turn into desert.

OvergrazingDesertification

Q57. hard

Why is maintaining biodiversity important for agriculture?

  1. A It keeps ecosystems balanced - pollinators, natural pest control and soil organisms all support crops
  2. B It is not important
  3. C It reduces yield
  4. D It only matters in forests

Answer: A — It keeps ecosystems balanced - pollinators, natural pest control and soil organisms all support crops

Bees and other insects pollinate crops, birds and predators control pests, and soil organisms recycle nutrients. Losing this variety would make farming far harder.

BiodiversityAgriculture

Q60. hard

Why is stubble (leftover crop) burning after harvest discouraged?

  1. A It causes heavy air pollution and destroys useful soil organisms and nutrients
  2. B It makes the soil richer
  3. C It saves fuel
  4. D It has no effect

Answer: A — It causes heavy air pollution and destroys useful soil organisms and nutrients

Burning crop residue releases large amounts of smoke and particulate matter into the air and kills the microbes and burns the organic matter that keep the soil fertile. Mixing the stubble back into the soil is far better.

Stubble burningPollution
