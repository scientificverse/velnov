Easy questions (2)

Q1. easy

The process by which living organisms produce new individuals of their own kind is called:

  1. A Reproduction
  2. B Respiration
  3. C Digestion
  4. D Excretion

Answer: A — Reproduction

Reproduction ensures the continuation of a species from one generation to the next. Without it, a species would disappear when its members die.

Reproduction

Q2. easy

The two main types of reproduction are:

  1. A Sexual and asexual
  2. B Fast and slow
  3. C Large and small
  4. D Land and water

Answer: A — Sexual and asexual

Asexual reproduction involves a single parent and no gametes; sexual reproduction involves two parents and the fusion of male and female gametes.

Types of reproduction

Medium questions (30)

Q3. medium

Reproduction that involves only ONE parent and no fusion of gametes is called:

  1. A Asexual reproduction
  2. B Sexual reproduction
  3. C Cross reproduction
  4. D External reproduction

Answer: A — Asexual reproduction

In asexual reproduction the offspring are produced from a single parent and are genetically identical to it. It is common in simple organisms and plants.

Asexual reproduction

Q4. medium

Reproduction that involves TWO parents and the fusion of gametes is called:

  1. A Sexual reproduction
  2. B Asexual reproduction
  3. C Budding
  4. D Fission

Answer: A — Sexual reproduction

Sexual reproduction combines genetic material from a male and a female parent, so the offspring show variation and are not identical to either parent.

Sexual reproduction

Q6. medium

The cell formed by the fusion of a male and a female gamete is called a:

  1. A Zygote
  2. B Gamete
  3. C Spore
  4. D Bud

Answer: A — Zygote

The zygote is the first cell of a new individual. It divides repeatedly to form an embryo, which grows into the offspring.

ZygoteFertilisation

Q8. medium

In Amoeba, the parent cell simply splits into two. This method of asexual reproduction is called:

  1. A Binary fission
  2. B Budding
  3. C Fragmentation
  4. D Spore formation

Answer: A — Binary fission

In binary fission, the nucleus divides first, then the cell splits into two daughter cells, each a complete new Amoeba.

Binary fissionAmoeba

Q10. medium

In yeast and Hydra, a small outgrowth develops and separates to form a new individual. This is called:

  1. A Budding
  2. B Binary fission
  3. C Fragmentation
  4. D Spore formation

Answer: A — Budding

A bud grows on the parent, develops into a small individual, and then detaches (or, in a colony, stays attached). It is a common form of asexual reproduction.

BuddingYeastHydra

Q12. medium

Fungi like Rhizopus (bread mould) and ferns reproduce by producing:

  1. A Spores
  2. B Buds
  3. C Seeds
  4. D Eggs

Answer: A — Spores

Spores are tiny, light reproductive units covered by a hard protective coat. They are carried by wind and germinate into new individuals when conditions are right.

Spore formationRhizopus

Q14. medium

The production of new plants from vegetative parts such as roots, stems and leaves is called:

  1. A Vegetative propagation
  2. B Sexual reproduction
  3. C Pollination
  4. D Fertilisation

Answer: A — Vegetative propagation

Vegetative propagation is a form of asexual reproduction in plants. Potato, ginger, sugarcane, rose and Bryophyllum all reproduce this way.

Vegetative propagation

Q16. medium

An ADVANTAGE of sexual reproduction is that:

  1. A It produces variation, helping the species adapt and survive
  2. B It is faster than asexual
  3. C It needs only one parent
  4. D Offspring are always identical

Answer: A — It produces variation, helping the species adapt and survive

By combining features from two parents, sexual reproduction creates variety. This variation helps some individuals survive changes in the environment.

Sexual reproductionVariation

Q18. medium

The transfer of pollen from the anther to the stigma is called:

  1. A Pollination
  2. B Fertilisation
  3. C Germination
  4. D Dispersal

Answer: A — Pollination

Pollination precedes fertilisation. It may occur within one flower (self-pollination) or between two flowers of the same kind (cross-pollination).

Pollination

Q20. medium

Animals that lay eggs are called:

  1. A Oviparous
  2. B Viviparous
  3. C Herbivorous
  4. D Carnivorous

Answer: A — Oviparous

Birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish and insects are oviparous - the young develop inside eggs laid outside the mother's body.

Oviparous

Q21. medium

Animals that give birth to LIVE young are called:

  1. A Viviparous
  2. B Oviparous
  3. C Amphibious
  4. D Nocturnal

Answer: A — Viviparous

Most mammals, including humans, cows and dogs, are viviparous. The young develop inside the mother and are born alive.

Viviparous

Q23. medium

Fertilisation that takes place INSIDE the female body, as in humans and birds, is called:

  1. A Internal fertilisation
  2. B External fertilisation
  3. C Budding
  4. D Fission

Answer: A — Internal fertilisation

In internal fertilisation, the sperm are deposited inside the female, where they fuse with the egg. Fewer eggs are needed because the chances of survival are higher.

Internal fertilisation

Q25. medium

The female reproductive cell in animals is the:

  1. A Egg (ovum)
  2. B Sperm
  3. C Pollen
  4. D Zygote

Answer: A — Egg (ovum)

The egg is larger than the sperm and non-motile, containing food for the early embryo. Eggs are produced in the female organs called the ovaries.

EggOvary

Q27. medium

The development of an embryo into a young one that resembles the adult is called:

  1. A Direct development
  2. B Metamorphosis
  3. C Fragmentation
  4. D Budding

Answer: A — Direct development

In direct development, the newborn looks like a small version of the adult - as in humans, cats and birds - and simply grows larger over time.

Direct development

Q29. medium

The life cycle of a frog passes through which stages?

  1. A Egg, tadpole (larva), and adult frog
  2. B Egg and adult only
  3. C Larva and pupa only
  4. D Egg, pupa, adult

Answer: A — Egg, tadpole (larva), and adult frog

A frog lays eggs in water. Each egg hatches into a tadpole with gills and a tail, which gradually grows legs and lungs and becomes an adult frog.

FrogMetamorphosis

Q31. medium

The ability of some animals to grow back a lost body part is called:

  1. A Regeneration
  2. B Reproduction
  3. C Metamorphosis
  4. D Budding

Answer: A — Regeneration

Hydra, Planaria and starfish can regrow lost parts. If a Planaria is cut into pieces, each piece may grow into a complete new organism.

Regeneration

Q33. medium

Cloning is the production of an organism that is:

  1. A An exact genetic copy of another organism
  2. B Very different from its parent
  3. C A mixture of two species
  4. D Made from two parents

Answer: A — An exact genetic copy of another organism

A clone is genetically identical to the single parent. Dolly the sheep, born in 1996, was the first mammal cloned from an adult body cell.

CloningDolly

Q35. medium

Why do fish and frogs lay a very LARGE number of eggs?

  1. A Many eggs are eaten or fail to develop, so a large number ensures some survive
  2. B It is a waste
  3. C The eggs are very small
  4. D To feed other animals only

Answer: A — Many eggs are eaten or fail to develop, so a large number ensures some survive

With external fertilisation and no parental care, most eggs and young are lost to predators or unfavourable conditions. Laying many eggs improves the chance that some grow into adults.

External fertilisationSurvival

Q37. medium

Which of these reproduces by SEEDS?

  1. A Mango tree
  2. B Amoeba
  3. C Bacteria
  4. D Yeast

Answer: A — Mango tree

Flowering plants such as mango reproduce sexually through seeds formed after pollination and fertilisation. Amoeba, bacteria and yeast reproduce asexually.

SeedsSexual reproduction

Q39. medium

The young one inside the mother's body develops into a baby and is born. The developing young is called:

  1. A An embryo (later a foetus)
  2. B A zygote only
  3. C A gamete
  4. D A spore

Answer: A — An embryo (later a foetus)

After fertilisation, the zygote divides to form an embryo, which develops organs and grows into a foetus before being born as a baby.

Q41. medium

Self-pollination occurs when pollen is transferred:

  1. A From the anther to the stigma of the same flower or another flower on the same plant
  2. B Between two different species
  3. C From a flower to a leaf
  4. D From root to flower

Answer: A — From the anther to the stigma of the same flower or another flower on the same plant

Self-pollination keeps the parent's characters but gives little variation. Cross-pollination between two plants of the same kind creates more variety.

Self-pollination

Q49. medium

Budding in yeast is best described as:

A tiny bud appears on the yeast cell, grows, and pinches off as a new individual. Under good conditions, chains of buds can form rapidly.

Q51. medium

The eggs of birds have a hard shell that:

  2. B Helps the egg float

The calcium-rich shell shields the embryo from injury and moisture loss while allowing air to pass through tiny pores for the embryo to breathe.

Bird eggsShell

Q53. medium

Layering is a method of propagation in which:

  1. A A branch is bent to the ground and covered with soil until it grows roots
  2. B Seeds are sown
  3. C The plant is cut into spores
  4. D Two plants are crossed

Answer: A — A branch is bent to the ground and covered with soil until it grows roots

In layering, a low branch is buried while still attached to the parent. It develops roots, after which it is cut off and grown as a separate plant. Jasmine and grapes are propagated this way.

LayeringVegetative propagation

Q55. medium

Why do farmers often prefer vegetative propagation for fruit plants?

  1. A The new plants are identical to the parent and bear fruit sooner than seed-grown plants
  2. B It produces more variety
  3. C It is always cheaper
  4. D Seeds never grow

Answer: A — The new plants are identical to the parent and bear fruit sooner than seed-grown plants

Because the offspring are genetically identical, the desirable fruit quality is preserved exactly, and the plants mature and fruit faster than those raised from seed.

Q57. medium

Q5. hard

The male and female reproductive cells are together called:

  1. A Gametes
  2. B Zygotes
  3. C Embryos
  4. D Spores

Answer: A — Gametes

The male gamete (sperm in animals, pollen in plants) fuses with the female gamete (egg or ovule). Their union begins the development of a new individual.

Gametes

Q7. hard

The fusion of the male and female gametes to form a zygote is called:

  1. A Fertilisation
  2. B Pollination
  3. C Germination
  4. D Budding

Answer: A — Fertilisation

Fertilisation is the central event of sexual reproduction. It restores the full number of chromosomes and combines features from both parents.

FertilisationZygote

Q9. hard

Plasmodium, the malarial parasite, reproduces by splitting into MANY individuals at once. This is called:

  1. A Multiple fission
  2. B Binary fission
  3. C Budding
  4. D Fragmentation

Answer: A — Multiple fission

In multiple fission, the nucleus divides many times inside the parent, and each part surrounds itself with cytoplasm, so many offspring form together.

Multiple fissionPlasmodium

  1. A Fragmentation
  2. B Budding

Answer: A — Fragmentation

Fragmentation is the breaking of the body into fragments, each of which grows into a complete organism. It is seen in Spirogyra and many other algae.

Q13. hard

  3. C Make food
  4. D Attract insects

Answer: A — Survive unfavourable conditions such as high heat or lack of moisture

The tough coat lets spores remain dormant through drought or extreme temperatures. When conditions improve, they germinate, which is why mould appears quickly on stale food.

Q58. hard

Which overall statement about reproduction is CORRECT?

  2. B Only plants reproduce
