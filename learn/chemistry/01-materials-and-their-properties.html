Easy questions (1)

Q1. easy

The substance from which an object is made is called its:

  1. A Material
  2. B Shape
  3. C Colour
  4. D Weight

Answer: A — Material

A material is the substance used to make an object. For example, a chair may be made of the material wood, plastic or metal.

Medium questions (31)

Q2. medium

Different objects can be made from the same material, and the same object can be made from different materials. A tumbler can be made of:

  1. A Glass, plastic, steel or paper
  2. B Only glass
  3. C Only plastic
  4. D Only metal

Answer: A — Glass, plastic, steel or paper

The same object (a tumbler) can be made from many materials - glass, plastic, steel, even paper. The material chosen depends on the properties needed, such as strength or transparency.

MaterialsObjects

Q3. medium

Materials that have a shine on their surface are said to have:

  1. A Lustre
  2. B Hardness
  3. C Solubility
  4. D Density

Answer: A — Lustre

Lustre is the shine seen on the surface of a material. Metals like gold, silver, copper and iron are lustrous, which is why they are polished to look shiny.

LustreMetals

Q5. medium

Materials through which things can be seen CLEARLY are called:

  1. A Transparent
  2. B Opaque
  3. C Translucent
  4. D Lustrous

Answer: A — Transparent

Transparent materials (glass, clear plastic, air, water) let light pass through completely, so objects behind them can be seen clearly.

TransparentLight

Q6. medium

Materials through which we CANNOT see at all are called:

  1. A Opaque
  2. B Transparent
  3. C Translucent
  4. D Soluble

Answer: A — Opaque

Opaque materials such as wood, cardboard, metal and stone do not allow light to pass through, so nothing can be seen through them.

OpaqueLight

Q8. medium

Materials that can be compressed or scratched easily are said to be:

  1. A Soft
  2. B Hard
  3. C Lustrous
  4. D Soluble

Answer: A — Soft

Soft materials like sponge, cotton and rubber can be compressed or scratched easily. Hard materials like iron and stone resist being scratched or compressed.

SoftnessHardness

Q10. medium

Substances that dissolve completely in water are said to be:

  1. A Soluble
  2. B Insoluble
  3. C Opaque
  4. D Lustrous

Answer: A — Soluble

Soluble substances like salt and sugar dissolve in water and disappear from view, forming a solution. Insoluble substances like sand and chalk powder do not dissolve.

SolubleSolution

Q11. medium

Which of the following is INSOLUBLE in water?

  1. A Sand
  2. B Sugar
  3. C Salt
  4. D Glucose

Answer: A — Sand

Sand does not dissolve in water - it settles at the bottom, so it is insoluble. Sugar, salt and glucose all dissolve, so they are soluble.

InsolubleSand

Q13. medium

Which of these liquids does NOT mix with water?

  1. A Coconut oil
  2. B Vinegar
  3. C Lemon juice
  4. D Milk

Answer: A — Coconut oil

Oil does not mix with water - it forms a separate layer on top because it is immiscible with water. Vinegar, lemon juice and milk mix with water.

ImmiscibleOil and water

Q15. medium

Objects that FLOAT on water are usually:

  1. A Lighter (less dense) than water
  2. B Heavier than water
  3. C Always made of metal
  4. D Always transparent

Answer: A — Lighter (less dense) than water

Objects less dense than water float, while denser objects sink. Wood, thermocol, plastic and oil float on water; iron nails and stones sink.

FloatingDensity

Q17. medium

Grouping materials with similar properties together is called:

  1. A Classification (sorting into groups)
  2. B Separation
  3. C Dissolving
  4. D Melting

Answer: A — Classification (sorting into groups)

Classification is grouping objects or materials based on shared properties. It makes study easier - for example, grouping all metals together, or all soluble substances together.

ClassificationGrouping

Metals like copper and aluminium are good conductors of electricity, so they are drawn into wires. Their ductility (ability to be drawn into wires) also helps.

  2. B Dull, soft and non-conducting

  4. D Always liquid

Answer: A — Lustrous, hard, malleable, ductile and good conductors

Most metals are lustrous (shiny), hard, malleable, ductile, sonorous, and good conductors of heat and electricity. Non-metals usually show the opposite properties.

  1. A Aluminium
  2. B Wood
  3. C Plastic
  4. D Rubber

Answer: A — Aluminium

Metals like aluminium, copper and iron conduct heat well, which is why cooking utensils are made of them. Wood, plastic and rubber are poor conductors (insulators).

Q26. medium

A solution formed when a substance dissolves in a liquid has the dissolving liquid called the:

  1. A Solvent
  2. B Solute
  3. C Mixture
  4. D Residue

Answer: A — Solvent

In a solution, the substance that dissolves is the solute (e.g., sugar) and the liquid in which it dissolves is the solvent (e.g., water). Together they form a solution.

SolventSoluteSolution

Q28. medium

A solution in which no more solute can dissolve at a given temperature is called:

  1. A A saturated solution
  2. B An unsaturated solution
  3. C A suspension
  4. D A mixture only

Answer: A — A saturated solution

A saturated solution holds the maximum amount of solute it can dissolve at that temperature. Adding more solute leaves it undissolved at the bottom.

Saturated solution

Q60. medium

Why are cooking vessels usually made of metal but their handles of plastic or wood?

  2. B Plastic is stronger than metal
  3. C Wood conducts heat better than metal

Answer: A — Metals conduct heat well for cooking, while plastic and wood are poor conductors and stay cool to hold

The vessel must transfer heat quickly to the food, so a good conductor like aluminium or steel is used. The handle must not transfer heat to the hand, so an insulator such as plastic or wood is chosen - a clear case of properties deciding uses.

Q4. hard

Metals lose their shine and look dull after some time because of:

  1. A The action of air and moisture on their surface
  2. B Sunlight only
  3. C Their weight increasing
  4. D Being touched

Answer: A — The action of air and moisture on their surface

Air and moisture react with a metal's surface, forming a dull layer (like rust on iron or tarnish on silver). Rubbing or polishing removes this layer and restores the shine.

TarnishLustre

  1. A Translucent
  2. B Transparent
  3. C Opaque
  4. D Lustrous

Answer: A — Translucent

Translucent materials let only some light through, so objects behind appear blurred and unclear. Butter paper, frosted glass and thin cloth are translucent.

Translucent

  1. A Diamond
  2. B Iron
  3. C Gold
  4. D Glass

Answer: A — Diamond

Diamond is the hardest natural substance. It is so hard that it can scratch every other material, which is why diamonds are used in cutting and drilling tools.

DiamondHardness

Q12. hard

Chalk powder mixed in water settles down after some time. This shows chalk is:

  1. A Insoluble in water
  2. B Soluble in water
  3. C A liquid
  4. D Transparent

Answer: A — Insoluble in water

Insoluble solids do not dissolve; their particles remain suspended and slowly settle to the bottom (sedimentation). Chalk powder behaves this way in water.

InsolubleSedimentation

Q14. hard

Liquids that do NOT mix with each other are called:

  1. A Immiscible liquids
  2. B Miscible liquids
  3. C Soluble liquids
  4. D Solutions

Answer: A — Immiscible liquids

Immiscible liquids (like oil and water) form separate layers instead of mixing. Miscible liquids (like water and vinegar) mix completely to form a single liquid.

ImmiscibleMiscible

Q16. hard

A huge iron ship floats on water while a small iron nail sinks. This is because the ship:

  1. A Is shaped to displace a large amount of water, making its overall density less than water
  2. B Is made of a lighter metal
  3. C Is painted
  4. D Moves fast

Answer: A — Is shaped to displace a large amount of water, making its overall density less than water

A ship is hollow and shaped to push aside (displace) a large volume of water. This makes its average density less than that of water, so it floats - even though iron itself is denser than water.

FloatingDensityShips
