Easy questions (2)

Q1. easy

Anything that has mass and occupies space is called:

  1. A Matter
  2. B Energy
  3. C Force
  4. D Light

Answer: A — Matter

Everything around us - a book, water, air, a stone - is matter, because it has mass and takes up space (volume). Light and heat are energy, not matter.

Matter

Q2. easy

The three common states of matter are:

  1. A Solid, liquid and gas
  2. B Hot, cold and warm
  3. C Big, small and medium
  4. D Hard, soft and liquid

Answer: A — Solid, liquid and gas

Matter exists mainly as solids, liquids and gases. Plasma is a fourth state found in stars and lightning.

States of matter

Medium questions (31)

Q3. medium

Which state of matter has a DEFINITE shape and a definite volume?

  1. A Solid
  2. B Liquid
  3. C Gas
  4. D None

Answer: A — Solid

In a solid, particles are tightly packed in fixed positions, so a solid keeps both its shape and its volume.

Solids

Q4. medium

Which state of matter has a definite VOLUME but no definite shape?

  1. A Liquid
  2. B Solid
  3. C Gas
  4. D Plasma

Answer: A — Liquid

A liquid takes the shape of its container but keeps the same volume, because its particles can slide past each other while staying close together.

Liquids

Q5. medium

Which state of matter has NEITHER a definite shape NOR a definite volume?

  1. A Gas
  2. B Solid
  3. C Liquid
  4. D Crystal

Answer: A — Gas

Gas particles are far apart and move freely, so a gas spreads out to fill whatever container it is in, taking both its shape and volume.

Gases

Q8. medium

The spreading out of a substance on its own, like the smell of perfume filling a room, is called:

  1. A Diffusion
  2. B Condensation
  3. C Sublimation
  4. D Filtration

Answer: A — Diffusion

Diffusion is the intermixing of particles due to their constant random motion. It is fastest in gases, slower in liquids and extremely slow in solids.

Diffusion

Q10. medium

Diffusion becomes FASTER when the temperature is:

  1. A Increased
  2. B Decreased
  3. C Kept constant
  4. D Temperature has no effect

Answer: A — Increased

Higher temperature gives particles more kinetic energy, so they move faster and mix more quickly. Sugar dissolves faster in hot tea than in cold water for the same reason.

DiffusionTemperature

Q12. medium

Which of these is an ELEMENT?

  1. A Copper
  2. B Water
  3. C Common salt
  4. D Air

Answer: A — Copper

Copper is made of only copper atoms. Water and salt are compounds made of two or more elements, and air is a mixture.

ElementClassification

Q14. medium

Which of these is a COMPOUND?

  1. A Water (H2O)
  2. B Oxygen
  3. C Air
  4. D Brass

Answer: A — Water (H2O)

Water is formed when hydrogen and oxygen combine chemically in a 2:1 ratio. Oxygen is an element, and air and brass are mixtures.

CompoundWater

Q16. medium

Which of these is a MIXTURE?

  1. A Air
  2. B Water
  3. C Carbon dioxide
  4. D Sodium chloride

Answer: A — Air

Air is a mixture of nitrogen, oxygen, argon, carbon dioxide and water vapour in varying proportions. The other three are compounds.

MixtureAir

Q18. medium

A mixture of iron filings and sulphur powder is a mixture, but heating them forms iron sulphide, which is:

  1. A A compound with completely different properties
  2. B Still a mixture
  3. C An element
  4. D Unchanged

Answer: A — A compound with completely different properties

In the mixture, a magnet still attracts the iron. After heating, the black iron sulphide formed is not attracted by a magnet - a new substance with new properties.

Iron sulphideCompound formation

Q20. medium

A mixture in which the different components can be SEEN separately is called:

  1. A A heterogeneous mixture
  2. B A homogeneous mixture
  3. C A solution
  4. D A compound

Answer: A — A heterogeneous mixture

Sand and water, oil and water, and a mixture of pulses are heterogeneous - the parts are visibly distinct and unevenly distributed.

Heterogeneous mixture

Q22. medium

In a salt solution, the SOLUTE is:

  1. A The salt (the substance that dissolves)
  2. B The water
  3. C The container
  4. D The air above

Answer: A — The salt (the substance that dissolves)

The solute is the substance that dissolves, and the solvent is the one that does the dissolving. Salt is the solute; water is the solvent.

SoluteSolvent

Q23. medium

Water is called the UNIVERSAL SOLVENT because it:

  1. A Dissolves a very large number of substances
  2. B Dissolves everything without exception
  3. C Is found everywhere
  4. D Is colourless

Answer: A — Dissolves a very large number of substances

Water dissolves more substances than any other liquid, which is why it carries nutrients in our bodies and in plants. It still cannot dissolve oil, wax or sand.

WaterUniversal solvent

Q25. medium

Milk is an example of:

  1. A A colloid
  2. B A true solution
  3. C An element
  4. D A compound

Answer: A — A colloid

Milk is a colloid - tiny fat droplets are dispersed in water. The particles are bigger than in a solution but too small to settle, so milk looks uniform but is cloudy.

ColloidMilk

Q27. medium

The smallest particle of an element that can take part in a chemical reaction is called:

  1. A An atom
  2. B A molecule
  3. C A mixture
  4. D A compound

Answer: A — An atom

Atoms are the basic building blocks of matter. They combine to form molecules and compounds.

Atom

Q29. medium

The chemical formula of water, H2O, tells us that one molecule of water contains:

  1. A Two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom
  2. B Two oxygen atoms and one hydrogen atom
  3. C Two atoms in total
  4. D Only hydrogen

Answer: A — Two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom

The subscript shows the number of atoms of each element. H2O means 2 hydrogen atoms bonded to 1 oxygen atom - always in that fixed ratio.

Chemical formulaWater

Q31. medium

The chemical symbol for IRON is:

  1. A Fe
  2. B Ir
  3. C In
  4. D I

Answer: A — Fe

Iron's symbol Fe comes from its Latin name ferrum. Ir is iridium and I is iodine.

Chemical symbolsIron

Q33. medium

The chemical formula of carbon dioxide is:

  1. A CO2
  2. B CO
  3. C C2O
  4. D CaO

Answer: A — CO2

Carbon dioxide has one carbon atom bonded to two oxygen atoms. CO is carbon monoxide, a very different and poisonous gas.

Q35. medium

The change from solid to liquid on heating is called:

  1. A Melting
  2. B Freezing
  3. C Boiling
  4. D Condensation

Answer: A — Melting

At the melting point, the particles gain enough energy to break free from their fixed positions and begin sliding over each other, turning the solid into a liquid.

Melting

Q36. medium

The change from liquid to solid on cooling is called:

  1. A Freezing (solidification)
  2. B Melting
  3. C Evaporation
  4. D Sublimation

Answer: A — Freezing (solidification)

As a liquid cools, its particles lose energy and settle into fixed positions, forming a solid. Water freezes into ice at 0 °C.

Freezing

Q38. medium

Evaporation differs from boiling because evaporation:

  1. A Takes place only at the surface and at any temperature
  2. B Happens only at the boiling point
  3. C Happens throughout the liquid
  4. D Needs no energy

Answer: A — Takes place only at the surface and at any temperature

Evaporation is a surface phenomenon that occurs at all temperatures, which is why wet clothes dry even in the shade. Boiling occurs throughout the liquid at a fixed temperature.

EvaporationBoiling

Q40. medium

Water in an earthen pot (matka) stays cool because:

  1. A Water seeps through the tiny pores and evaporates, taking heat from the pot
  2. B The clay is cold
  3. C The pot is airtight
  4. D Clay produces ice

Answer: A — Water seeps through the tiny pores and evaporates, taking heat from the pot

The porous clay lets a little water reach the outer surface, where it evaporates. The evaporation draws heat from the remaining water, keeping it cool.

EvaporationEarthen pot

Q42. medium

Clothes dry faster on a windy day because wind:

  1. A Carries away the water vapour, allowing more evaporation
  2. B Heats the clothes
  3. C Adds moisture
  4. D Freezes the water

Answer: A — Carries away the water vapour, allowing more evaporation

Moving air removes the humid layer just above the wet cloth and replaces it with drier air, so evaporation can continue rapidly.

EvaporationWind

Q44. medium

When sugar dissolves in a full glass of water, the water does NOT overflow because:

  1. A Sugar particles fit into the spaces between water particles
  2. B The sugar disappears
  3. C The water evaporates
  4. D The glass expands

Answer: A — Sugar particles fit into the spaces between water particles

There are gaps between water particles, and the much smaller dissolved sugar particles occupy them - direct evidence that matter is particulate with spaces in between.

Particle theoryDissolving

Q46. medium

Alloys such as brass and steel are classified as:

  1. A Homogeneous mixtures (solid solutions)
  2. B Compounds
  3. C Elements
  4. D Heterogeneous mixtures

Answer: A — Homogeneous mixtures (solid solutions)

Alloys are uniform throughout and have no fixed composition ratio, so they are homogeneous mixtures - specifically, solid solutions of metals.

AlloysHomogeneous mixtures

Q48. medium

Elements are broadly classified into:

  1. A Metals, non-metals and metalloids
  2. B Solids and liquids only
  3. C Acids and bases
  4. D Mixtures and compounds

Answer: A — Metals, non-metals and metalloids

Metals are lustrous conductors, non-metals are mostly dull insulators, and metalloids such as silicon show properties of both.

Classification of elements

Q50. medium

The components of a compound can be separated only by:

  1. A Chemical methods
  2. B Filtration
  3. C Handpicking
  4. D Sieving

Answer: A — Chemical methods

Because the elements in a compound are chemically bonded, physical methods cannot separate them. Electrolysis or heating with a suitable reagent is needed.

CompoundSeparation

Q52. medium

Which of these shows that air is a MIXTURE and not a compound?

  1. A Its components are present in varying proportions and can be separated physically
  2. B It is invisible
  3. C It has mass
  4. D It occupies space

Answer: A — Its components are present in varying proportions and can be separated physically

The proportion of gases in air varies from place to place, no chemical reaction happens when they mix, and fractional distillation of liquid air separates them physically.

AirMixture

Q54. medium

Dry ice is solid carbon dioxide. It is called 'dry' because it:

  1. A Sublimes directly into gas without becoming a liquid
  2. B Contains no carbon
  3. C Is very hot
  4. D Absorbs water

Answer: A — Sublimes directly into gas without becoming a liquid

Unlike ordinary ice, dry ice leaves no puddle - it turns straight into carbon dioxide gas, which is why it is used to keep things frozen and to create stage fog.

Dry iceSublimation

Q56. medium

During melting, the temperature of ice stays at 0 °C even though heat is being supplied because:

  1. A The heat is used to break the forces holding the particles in fixed positions (latent heat)
  2. B The thermometer is faulty
  3. C No heat enters the ice
  4. D Ice cannot absorb heat

Answer: A — The heat is used to break the forces holding the particles in fixed positions (latent heat)

The energy supplied during a change of state goes into overcoming the attraction between particles rather than raising their speed, so the temperature stays constant. This is latent heat.

Latent heatMelting

Q58. medium

Which of these is the correct classification of MATTER?

  1. A Matter → pure substances (elements, compounds) and mixtures (homogeneous, heterogeneous)
  2. B Matter → only elements
  3. C Matter → only mixtures
  4. D Matter → acids and bases only

Answer: A — Matter → pure substances (elements, compounds) and mixtures (homogeneous, heterogeneous)

Matter divides first into pure substances and mixtures. Pure substances are elements or compounds; mixtures are homogeneous (solutions) or heterogeneous (suspensions and others).

Classification of matter

Q60. medium

Why can you smell food cooking in the kitchen from another room?

  1. A Vapour particles from the food diffuse through the air and reach your nose
  2. B Sound carries the smell
  3. C Light carries the smell
  4. D The walls produce the smell

Answer: A — Vapour particles from the food diffuse through the air and reach your nose

Particles of the aromatic substances leave the hot food, mix with the constantly moving air particles and spread through the house by diffusion - everyday evidence that matter is made of moving particles.

Hard questions (27)

Q6. hard

Gases can be compressed easily because:

  1. A The spaces between gas particles are very large
  2. B Gas particles are heavy
  3. C Gas particles are tightly packed
  4. D Gases have no particles

Answer: A — The spaces between gas particles are very large

Large gaps between particles allow them to be pushed closer together. This is why LPG and CNG can be compressed into cylinders, while liquids and solids cannot.

CompressibilityGases

Q7. hard

The force of attraction between particles is STRONGEST in:

  1. A Solids
  2. B Liquids
  3. C Gases
  4. D It is the same in all

Answer: A — Solids

Solids have the strongest interparticle forces, which hold the particles in fixed positions. The forces are weaker in liquids and almost negligible in gases.

Interparticle forces

Q9. hard

Diffusion is FASTEST in gases because gas particles:

  1. A Move very fast and have large spaces between them
  2. B Are held tightly
  3. C Do not move
  4. D Are very heavy

Answer: A — Move very fast and have large spaces between them

With high speeds and plenty of empty space, gas particles spread through one another quickly. In solids, fixed particles allow almost no diffusion.

DiffusionGases

Q11. hard

A pure substance made up of only ONE kind of atom is called:

  1. A An element
  2. B A compound
  3. C A mixture
  4. D A solution

Answer: A — An element

Elements such as oxygen, gold, iron and carbon cannot be broken down into simpler substances by any chemical process.

Element

Q13. hard

A pure substance formed when two or more elements combine CHEMICALLY in a fixed ratio is called:

  1. A A compound
  2. B An element
  3. C A mixture
  4. D An alloy

Answer: A — A compound

In a compound, the elements are chemically bonded in a definite proportion, and the compound's properties are quite different from those of its constituent elements.

Compound

Q15. hard

A substance made of two or more substances mixed physically, in any proportion, is called:

  1. A A mixture
  2. B A compound
  3. C An element
  4. D An atom

Answer: A — A mixture

In a mixture the components keep their own properties and can be present in any ratio. They can usually be separated by physical methods.

Mixture

Q17. hard

Which statement correctly distinguishes a compound from a mixture?

  1. A A compound has a fixed composition and new properties; a mixture has variable composition and keeps its components' properties
  2. B Both have fixed composition
  3. C A compound can be separated by filtration
  4. D A mixture always needs a chemical reaction to form

Answer: A — A compound has a fixed composition and new properties; a mixture has variable composition and keeps its components' properties

Making a compound is a chemical change with heat or light often given out, and the product behaves nothing like its elements. Making a mixture is just physical mixing.

Compound vs mixture

Q19. hard

A mixture that is UNIFORM throughout, with the same composition everywhere, is called:

  1. A A homogeneous mixture
  2. B A heterogeneous mixture
  3. C A compound
  4. D A suspension

Answer: A — A homogeneous mixture

Salt solution, sugar solution, air and alloys are homogeneous - you cannot see separate parts, and every sample has the same composition.

Homogeneous mixture

Q21. hard

A homogeneous mixture of a solute dissolved in a solvent is called:

  1. A A solution
  2. B A suspension
  3. C A colloid
  4. D A compound

Answer: A — A solution

In a solution, the solute particles are so small that they cannot be seen or filtered out, and the mixture is transparent and uniform - like salt in water.

Solution

Q24. hard

A mixture in which solid particles are visible and settle down on standing is called:

  1. A A suspension
  2. B A solution
  3. C A compound
  4. D An element

Answer: A — A suspension

In a suspension, such as chalk powder in water or muddy water, the particles are large enough to be seen, to scatter light and to settle out. They can be filtered.

Suspension

Q26. hard

Which of these can be separated by FILTRATION?

  1. A A suspension of chalk in water
  2. B A solution of sugar in water
  3. C Air
  4. D A salt solution

Answer: A — A suspension of chalk in water

Filtration removes particles that are undissolved. Suspensions can be filtered; true solutions cannot, because the dissolved particles pass through the filter paper.

FiltrationSuspension

Q28. hard

A group of two or more atoms chemically bonded together is called:

  1. A A molecule
  2. B An atom
  3. C An element
  4. D A mixture

Answer: A — A molecule

A molecule may contain atoms of the same element (O2, N2) or of different elements (H2O, CO2). It is the smallest unit of a substance that can exist independently.

Molecule

Q37. hard

The temperature at which a liquid changes into vapour throughout its bulk is called its:

  1. A Boiling point
  2. B Melting point
  3. C Freezing point
  4. D Ignition temperature

Answer: A — Boiling point

At the boiling point, vapour bubbles form throughout the liquid, not just at the surface. Water boils at 100 °C at normal atmospheric pressure.

Boiling point

Q39. hard

Evaporation causes COOLING because:

  1. A The escaping particles take heat energy away from the liquid and its surroundings
  2. B It adds heat
  3. C It freezes the liquid
  4. D It has no thermal effect

Answer: A — The escaping particles take heat energy away from the liquid and its surroundings

Only the most energetic particles escape, and they draw the latent heat needed from what is left behind. This is why sweating cools us and why water stays cool in an earthen pot.

EvaporationCooling effect

Q41. hard

Which factor does NOT increase the rate of evaporation?

  1. A Increasing the humidity of the air
  2. B Increasing the temperature
  3. C Increasing the surface area
  4. D Increasing the wind speed

Answer: A — Increasing the humidity of the air

Humid air already holds a lot of water vapour, so it accepts less, slowing evaporation. Heat, a larger surface and moving air all speed it up.

EvaporationFactors

Q43. hard

Matter is made up of tiny particles that:

  1. A Have spaces between them and are in constant motion
  2. B Are perfectly still
  3. C Touch each other with no gaps in any state
  4. D Cannot move at all

Answer: A — Have spaces between them and are in constant motion

These are the key ideas of the particle theory of matter. Constant motion explains diffusion, and the spaces between particles explain dissolving and compressibility.

Particle theory

Q45. hard

Which of these is a pure substance?

  1. A Distilled water
  2. B Sea water
  3. C Milk
  4. D Air

Answer: A — Distilled water

Distilled water contains only water molecules, so it is pure. Sea water, milk and air are all mixtures containing several different substances.

Pure substances

Q47. hard

How many elements are known at present (approximately)?

  1. A About 118
  2. B About 50
  3. C About 500
  4. D About 1000

Answer: A — About 118

About 118 elements are known, of which roughly 92 occur naturally. The rest have been made artificially in laboratories.

ElementsNumber

Q49. hard

Which of these correctly describes the properties of a compound compared with its elements?

  1. A The compound's properties are entirely different from those of the elements it contains
  2. B The compound has the average properties of its elements
  3. C The compound keeps all the elements' properties
  4. D There is no difference

Answer: A — The compound's properties are entirely different from those of the elements it contains

Sodium is a violently reactive metal and chlorine a poisonous gas, yet sodium chloride is edible table salt. Chemical combination creates entirely new properties.

Compound properties

Q51. hard

The process of splitting water into hydrogen and oxygen using electricity is called:

  1. A Electrolysis
  2. B Filtration
  3. C Evaporation
  4. D Sublimation

Answer: A — Electrolysis

Electrolysis passes an electric current through water, decomposing it into hydrogen at the negative electrode and oxygen at the positive one, in a 2:1 volume ratio.

ElectrolysisWater

Q53. hard

Sublimation is the change of state from:

  1. A Solid directly to gas, without passing through the liquid state
  2. B Liquid to gas
  3. C Gas to liquid
  4. D Solid to liquid

Answer: A — Solid directly to gas, without passing through the liquid state

Camphor, naphthalene, ammonium chloride, iodine and dry ice sublime. The reverse change, gas to solid, is called deposition.

Sublimation

Q55. hard

The temperature at which a solid melts and the temperature at which the liquid freezes are:

  1. A The same for a given substance
  2. B Always different
  3. C Unrelated
  4. D Always 0 °C

Answer: A — The same for a given substance

Melting point and freezing point are the same temperature approached from opposite directions. For water, both are 0 °C at normal pressure.

Melting pointFreezing point

Q57. hard

Which sequence correctly shows increasing separation between particles?

  1. A Solid < Liquid < Gas
  2. B Gas < Liquid < Solid
  3. C Liquid < Solid < Gas
  4. D All are equal

Answer: A — Solid < Liquid < Gas

Particles are closest in solids, slightly further apart in liquids, and very widely spaced in gases. This explains their densities, compressibility and rates of diffusion.

Particle arrangementStates

Q59. hard

Which overall statement about matter is CORRECT?

  1. A Matter is made of tiny moving particles, exists as solid, liquid or gas, and is classified into elements, compounds and mixtures
  2. B Matter has no particles
  3. C All matter is a compound
  4. D Matter cannot change state

Answer: A — Matter is made of tiny moving particles, exists as solid, liquid or gas, and is classified into elements, compounds and mixtures

This sums up the topic: the particle nature of matter explains states, diffusion, dissolving and changes of state; and every sample of matter is either a pure substance (element or compound) or a mixture.
