Easy questions (2)

Q1. easy

Fibres made by human beings from chemicals are called:

  1. A Synthetic fibres
  2. B Natural fibres
  3. C Animal fibres
  4. D Plant fibres

Answer: A — Synthetic fibres

Synthetic (man-made) fibres such as nylon, polyester, rayon and acrylic are produced from chemicals, mostly petrochemicals. Cotton, wool, silk and jute are natural.

Synthetic fibres

Q2. easy

Which of these is a NATURAL fibre?

  1. A Cotton
  2. B Nylon
  3. C Polyester
  4. D Acrylic

Answer: A — Cotton

Cotton comes from the cotton plant, wool from sheep, silk from silkworms and jute from the jute plant. Nylon, polyester and acrylic are synthetic.

Natural fibres

Medium questions (30)

Q3. medium

A synthetic fibre is made of many small units joined together. Each small unit is called a:

  1. A Monomer
  2. B Polymer
  3. C Fibre
  4. D Atom

Answer: A — Monomer

Monomers are the repeating small molecules. When many join into a long chain, the result is a polymer - the basic structure of all synthetic fibres and plastics.

MonomerPolymer

Q4. medium

A large molecule made by joining many small repeating units is called a:

  1. A Polymer
  2. B Monomer
  3. C Element
  4. D Mixture

Answer: A — Polymer

The word polymer comes from Greek: poly means many and mer means part. Cellulose in cotton is a natural polymer; nylon and polyester are synthetic polymers.

Polymer

Answer: A — Is made from natural wood pulp but resembles silk in appearance

Rayon is obtained by chemically treating wood pulp. Because it has a silk-like lustre and feel but is far cheaper, it is called artificial silk.

  1. A Cotton (cellulose)
  2. B Nylon
  3. C Polyester
  4. D Acrylic

Answer: A — Cotton (cellulose)

Cotton is cellulose, a natural polymer of glucose. This shows polymers occur in nature too - synthetic polymers simply copy the idea using chemicals.

CelluloseNatural polymer

Q25. hard

Plastics that can be softened repeatedly by heating and remoulded are called:

  1. A Thermoplastics
  2. B Thermosetting plastics
  3. C Fibres
  4. D Alloys

Answer: A — Thermoplastics

Thermoplastics such as polythene and PVC have linear chains that slide over each other on heating, so they can be melted and reshaped many times.

Thermoplastic

Q26. hard

Plastics that once moulded CANNOT be softened again by heating are called:

  1. A Thermosetting plastics
  2. B Thermoplastics
  3. C Rayon
  4. D Nylon

Answer: A — Thermosetting plastics

Thermosetting plastics like bakelite and melamine have cross-linked chains that lock permanently on setting. They cannot be remoulded and will char rather than melt.

Thermosetting plastics
