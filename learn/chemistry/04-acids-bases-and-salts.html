Easy questions (2)

Q1. easy

Substances that taste SOUR and turn blue litmus red are called:

  1. A Acids
  2. B Bases
  3. C Salts
  4. D Neutral substances

Answer: A — Acids

Acids are sour in taste and turn blue litmus paper red. Lemon juice, vinegar and tamarind are common acidic substances. (Never taste laboratory chemicals.)

AcidsLitmus

Q2. easy

Substances that taste BITTER, feel soapy and turn red litmus blue are called:

  1. A Bases
  2. B Acids
  3. C Salts
  4. D Indicators

Answer: A — Bases

Bases are bitter, soapy to touch and turn red litmus blue. Baking soda solution, lime water and soap solution are basic.

BasesLitmus

The word ACID comes from a Latin word meaning:

  1. A Sour
  2. B Bitter
  3. C Sweet
  4. D Salty

Answer: A — Sour

'Acid' comes from the Latin word acidus/acere, meaning sour - reflecting the characteristic sour taste of acidic substances.

AcidsOrigin of word

Q4. medium

A substance used to test whether a solution is acidic or basic is called:

  1. A An indicator
  2. B A solvent
  3. C A solute
  4. D A catalyst

Answer: A — An indicator

Indicators change colour in the presence of acids or bases. Litmus, turmeric, china rose and phenolphthalein are common indicators.

Indicator

Q27. medium

Acid + Base  →  Salt + Water  (+ heat)
  1. A Neutralisation
  2. B Combustion
  3. C Displacement
  4. D Sublimation

Answer: A — Neutralisation

In neutralisation, the acid and base cancel each other's effects, producing salt and water and usually releasing heat. It is a chemical change.

Neutralisation

Answer: A — Is basic and neutralises the excess acid in the soil

Quicklime (calcium oxide) or slaked lime (calcium hydroxide) is basic, so it neutralises acidic soil and brings the pH back to a range suitable for crops.

Decaying organic matter releases acids that neutralise excess alkalinity. Adding compost or manure is the usual remedy for basic soil.

Q59. medium

Which overall statement about acids, bases and salts is CORRECT?

  1. A Acids turn blue litmus red, bases turn red litmus blue, and together they neutralise to form a salt and water
  2. B Acids and bases never react
  3. C All salts are acidic
  4. D Indicators have no use

Answer: A — Acids turn blue litmus red, bases turn red litmus blue, and together they neutralise to form a salt and water

This summarises the topic: acids are sour and turn blue litmus red; bases are bitter, soapy and turn red litmus blue; indicators detect them; and neutralisation of an acid by a base gives salt, water and heat.

Soap solution is basic, and turmeric acts as an indicator that turns reddish-brown in bases. Washing with plenty of water removes the base and the stain fades back to yellow.

Q28. hard

  1. A Exothermic - heat is released
  2. B Endothermic - heat is absorbed
  3. C Without any energy change

Answer: A — Exothermic - heat is released

When an acid reacts with a base, heat is given out, so the mixture becomes warmer. This is why neutralisation is described as an exothermic reaction.

Q38. hard

  2. B The temperature of a solution
  3. C The density of a solution
