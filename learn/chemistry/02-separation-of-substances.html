Easy questions (2)

Q1. easy

Why do we need to separate the components of a mixture?

  1. A To remove impurities and obtain useful or pure substances
  2. B To make the mixture heavier
  3. C To change its colour
  4. D There is no need

Answer: A — To remove impurities and obtain useful or pure substances

We separate mixtures to remove harmful or useless components (like stones from rice), to obtain a useful component (like butter from milk), or to get a pure substance (like salt from sea water).

SeparationPurpose

Q2. easy

Picking out small stones from rice or wheat by hand is called:

  1. A Handpicking
  2. B Sieving
  3. C Winnowing
  4. D Filtration

Answer: A — Handpicking

Handpicking is used when the unwanted particles are few, and large enough in size or different enough in colour to be picked out by hand.

Handpicking

Medium questions (30)

Q3. medium

Handpicking is suitable only when the impurities are:

  1. A Few in number and clearly different in size or colour
  2. B Very tiny and numerous
  3. C Dissolved in water
  4. D Gases

Answer: A — Few in number and clearly different in size or colour

Handpicking works only when the impurities are large enough to be seen and picked, and few enough to make it practical. Tiny or dissolved impurities need other methods.

HandpickingLimitations

Q4. medium

The process of beating out grain stalks to separate the grain from the stalk is called:

  1. A Threshing
  2. B Winnowing
  3. C Sieving
  4. D Handpicking

Answer: A — Threshing

Threshing separates grain seeds from the harvested stalks by beating them, either manually or using machines and animals. It is done after harvesting.

ThreshingFarming

Q5. medium

The method of separating lighter husk from heavier grain using wind or air is called:

  1. A Winnowing
  2. B Threshing
  3. C Filtration
  4. D Sedimentation

Answer: A — Winnowing

In winnowing, the mixture is dropped from a height in the wind. The lighter husk is blown away and forms a separate heap, while the heavier grain falls straight down.

WinnowingWind

Q7. medium

Which method is used to separate components of a mixture that differ in the SIZE of their particles?

  1. A Sieving
  2. B Winnowing
  3. C Evaporation
  4. D Condensation

Answer: A — Sieving

Sieving passes a mixture through a sieve with holes of a chosen size. Smaller particles pass through while larger ones stay behind - as when flour is sieved to remove bran.

SievingParticle size

  1. A Fine sand from pebbles and stones
  2. B Salt from water
  3. C Oil from water
  4. D Iron from sulphur

Answer: A — Fine sand from pebbles and stones

At construction sites, sand is passed through a large sieve to separate the fine sand from pebbles and stones, since they differ greatly in particle size.

SievingConstruction

Q11. medium

Sedimentation followed by decantation is used to separate:

  1. A An insoluble solid from a liquid (like sand from water)
  2. B Salt from water
  3. C Two gases
  4. D Sugar from water

Answer: A — An insoluble solid from a liquid (like sand from water)

This pair of methods separates a heavier insoluble solid from a liquid. Dissolved substances like salt and sugar cannot be separated this way.

SedimentationDecantation

Q12. medium

Decantation can also separate two liquids that:

  1. A Do not mix with each other (like oil and water)
  2. B Mix completely
  3. C Are both gases
  4. D Are both solids

Answer: A — Do not mix with each other (like oil and water)

Immiscible liquids like oil and water form separate layers. The upper layer (oil) can be poured off, or a separating funnel can be used to drain the lower layer.

DecantationImmiscible liquids

Q13. medium

The process of removing insoluble solids from a liquid by passing it through a filter is called:

  1. A Filtration
  2. B Evaporation
  3. C Condensation
  4. D Winnowing

Answer: A — Filtration

Filtration passes a mixture through filter paper or a cloth. The liquid passes through while the insoluble solid particles are trapped, giving a much cleaner separation than decantation.

Filtration

Using a tea strainer traps the tea leaves (residue) while letting the liquid tea (filtrate) pass through - a common example of filtration at home.

FiltrationTea strainer

Q18. medium

The process of converting a liquid into its vapour by heating is called:

  1. A Evaporation
  2. B Condensation
  3. C Filtration
  4. D Sedimentation

Answer: A — Evaporation

Evaporation is the change of a liquid into vapour. It is used to recover a dissolved solid - the liquid evaporates away, leaving the solid behind.

Evaporation

Salt is obtained from sea water mainly by:

  1. A Evaporation of the water using sunlight

Sea water is trapped in shallow pits and left in the sun. The water evaporates, leaving the salt behind as a solid, which is then collected and purified.

Q24. medium

Churning milk or curd to separate butter uses the method of:

  1. A Churning (centrifugation)

  3. C Sieving

Answer: A — Churning (centrifugation)

Churning spins the mixture so that the lighter butter (fat) particles separate and collect at the top, while the heavier liquid (buttermilk) stays below. This is a form of centrifugation.

ChurningButter

Q26. medium

A mixture of iron filings and sand can best be separated using:

  1. A A magnet
  2. B Filtration
  3. C Evaporation
  4. D Winnowing

Answer: A — A magnet

Iron is magnetic, so a magnet attracts and removes the iron filings, leaving the non-magnetic sand behind. This is called magnetic separation.

Q30. medium

Which of these substances SUBLIMES?

  1. A Naphthalene (moth balls)
  2. B Common salt
  3. C Sand
  4. D Sugar

Answer: A — Naphthalene (moth balls)

Naphthalene balls slowly turn directly into vapour and shrink over time - they sublime. Camphor, ammonium chloride and iodine also sublime. Salt, sand and sugar do not.

SublimationNaphthalene

Q31. medium

A solution in which no more solute can dissolve at a given temperature is called:

  1. A A saturated solution
  2. B An unsaturated solution
  3. C A suspension
  4. D A colloid

Answer: A — A saturated solution

A saturated solution has dissolved the maximum possible solute at that temperature. Any extra solute added simply settles at the bottom undissolved.

Saturated solution

Q33. medium

The amount of a substance that can dissolve in a given amount of solvent is called its:

  1. A Solubility
  2. B Density
  3. C Lustre
  4. D Hardness

Answer: A — Solubility

Solubility measures how much of a solute can dissolve in a fixed quantity of solvent at a given temperature. Different substances have different solubilities.

Solubility

Q35. medium

  1. A Loading

Loading is the addition of a substance (usually alum) that makes fine suspended particles heavier so they settle faster, making the water easier to clarify.

Q37. medium

Which method separates two MISCIBLE liquids with different boiling points?

  1. A Distillation (fractional distillation)
  2. B Filtration
  3. C Decantation
  4. D Magnetic separation

Answer: A — Distillation (fractional distillation)

Miscible liquids that mix completely cannot be decanted. Distillation separates them by boiling: the liquid with the lower boiling point vaporises first and is condensed and collected separately.

DistillationMiscible liquids

Q49. medium

Which of the following CANNOT be separated by filtration?

  2. B Sand in water

Dissolved sugar passes through the filter with the water because its particles are far too small to be trapped. Only undissolved solids like sand, chalk and tea leaves are separated by filtration.

Answer: A — Two immiscible liquids like oil and water

In a separating funnel, immiscible liquids form two layers. The tap at the bottom is opened to drain the lower (denser) liquid, and closed just as the upper layer reaches it.

Separating funnelImmiscible liquids

Q53. medium

Which of these separation methods requires HEATING?

  1. A Evaporation, distillation and sublimation
  2. B Handpicking and sieving
  3. C Winnowing and threshing
  4. D Decantation and filtration

Answer: A — Evaporation, distillation and sublimation

Evaporation, distillation and sublimation all require heat to change a substance into vapour. The other methods are purely physical and need no heating.

Q55. medium

Crystallisation gives purer crystals than simple evaporation because:

  1. A Impurities remain dissolved in the liquid instead of drying onto the solid
  2. B It uses more heat
  3. C It is faster
  4. D It uses filtration only

Answer: A — Impurities remain dissolved in the liquid instead of drying onto the solid

In simple evaporation, everything dissolved dries out together, including impurities. In crystallisation, only the pure substance forms crystals while impurities stay in the remaining solution.

CrystallisationPurity

We choose a method based on how the components differ - in size, weight, solubility, magnetism, boiling point or state. Understanding these differences lets us pick the right technique.

Sedimentation is the settling of heavier insoluble solid particles (the sediment) at the bottom when a mixture is left undisturbed - for example, sand settling in muddy water.

  1. A Decantation

Decantation is carefully pouring off the clear upper liquid without disturbing the settled sediment. It usually follows sedimentation.

Simple evaporation recovers only the dissolved solid, letting the liquid escape. Distillation recovers both - the pure liquid is condensed and collected, and the solid stays behind.
