Easy questions (2)

Q1. easy

Which of the following is a property of MOST metals?

  1. A They are shiny, hard, malleable and good conductors
  2. B They are dull and brittle
  3. C They are poor conductors of heat
  4. D They are always liquid

Answer: A — They are shiny, hard, malleable and good conductors

Metals typically show lustre, hardness, malleability, ductility, sonority and good conduction of heat and electricity. Non-metals generally show the opposite properties.

MetalsProperties

Q2. easy

The shine seen on the surface of a freshly cut metal is called:

  1. A Metallic lustre
  2. B Malleability
  3. C Ductility
  4. D Sonority

Answer: A — Metallic lustre

Metallic lustre is the characteristic shine of metals. Gold, silver and freshly cut sodium all show it, though many metals dull on exposure to air.

Lustre

Medium questions (31)

Q3. medium

The property by which metals can be beaten into thin SHEETS is called:

  1. A Malleability
  2. B Ductility
  3. C Sonority
  4. D Conductivity

Answer: A — Malleability

Malleability lets metals like aluminium and gold be hammered into thin sheets - for example, aluminium foil used to wrap food.

Malleability

Q4. medium

The property by which metals can be drawn into thin WIRES is called:

  1. A Ductility
  2. B Malleability
  3. C Hardness
  4. D Sonority

Answer: A — Ductility

Ductility allows metals like copper and aluminium to be drawn into wires. This is why electrical wiring is made from these metals.

Ductility

Q5. medium

The property by which metals produce a ringing sound when struck is called:

  1. A Sonority
  2. B Ductility
  3. C Lustre
  4. D Malleability

Answer: A — Sonority

Sonorous metals ring when struck, which is why school bells, temple bells and cymbals are made of metal. Non-metals are not sonorous.

SonorityBells

Q7. medium

Electrical wires are usually made of copper or aluminium because these metals are:

  1. A Good conductors of electricity and ductile
  2. B Brittle and dull
  3. C Poor conductors
  4. D Very heavy

Answer: A — Good conductors of electricity and ductile

Copper and aluminium conduct electricity well and can be drawn into long thin wires. They are also reasonably cheap and resistant to corrosion.

CopperAluminiumWires

Q10. medium

Sodium and potassium are stored under kerosene because:

  1. A They react violently with air and moisture, sometimes catching fire
  2. B They dissolve in kerosene
  3. C Kerosene makes them shiny
  4. D They evaporate otherwise

Answer: A — They react violently with air and moisture, sometimes catching fire

These metals react vigorously with oxygen and water vapour in the air and can catch fire. Kerosene keeps air and moisture away from their surface.

SodiumStorageReactivity

Q14. medium

Non-metals are generally:

  1. A Dull, brittle and poor conductors of heat and electricity
  2. B Shiny and malleable
  3. C Always liquid
  4. D Sonorous

Answer: A — Dull, brittle and poor conductors of heat and electricity

Solid non-metals like sulphur and phosphorus break into pieces when hammered - they are brittle. They also lack lustre and do not conduct well.

Non-metalsBrittle

Which non-metal is essential for breathing and burning?

  1. A Oxygen

  4. D Carbon

Answer: A — Oxygen

Oxygen supports respiration in living beings and combustion of fuels. It makes up about 21% of the air.

NitrogenFertilisers

Q20. medium

When a metal burns in air (oxygen), it forms:

Metal + Oxygen  →  Metal oxide  (basic)
  1. A A metal oxide, which is basic in nature
  2. B An acid
  3. C A neutral gas
  4. D A salt only

Answer: A — A metal oxide, which is basic in nature

Metals combine with oxygen to give metal oxides. These dissolve in water to form bases, so metal oxides are described as basic oxides.

Metal oxidesBasic

Q21. medium

When a non-metal burns in air (oxygen), it forms:

Non-metal + Oxygen  →  Non-metal oxide  (acidic)
  1. A A non-metal oxide, which is acidic in nature
  2. B A base
  3. C A metal
  4. D A neutral salt

Answer: A — A non-metal oxide, which is acidic in nature

Non-metals form oxides that dissolve in water to give acids. For example, sulphur dioxide dissolves to form sulphurous acid, so non-metal oxides are acidic.

Non-metal oxidesAcidic

Q23. medium

Metals react with dilute acids to produce:

  1. A A salt and hydrogen gas
  2. B A salt and oxygen gas
  3. C Water only
  4. D Carbon dioxide

Answer: A — A salt and hydrogen gas

Reactive metals displace hydrogen from acids: Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl2 + H2. The hydrogen released burns with a pop sound.

Metal-acid reactionHydrogen

Q25. medium

Which metal reacts so vigorously with water that it catches fire?

  1. A Sodium
  2. B Iron
  3. C Copper
  4. D Gold

Answer: A — Sodium

Sodium reacts violently with cold water, releasing hydrogen and enough heat to set it alight. Iron reacts only with steam, and copper and gold do not react with water at all.

SodiumWater reaction

Q28. medium

A more reactive metal can displace a less reactive metal from its salt solution. This is called:

  1. A A displacement reaction
  2. B A decomposition reaction
  3. C Neutralisation
  4. D Sublimation

Answer: A — A displacement reaction

For example, iron placed in copper sulphate solution displaces copper: Fe + CuSO4 → FeSO4 + Cu. The blue solution turns green and copper deposits on the iron.

DisplacementReactivity

Aluminium has a low density but good strength, especially when alloyed. It also resists corrosion, making it ideal for aircraft and vehicle bodies.

Gold and silver are highly unreactive, so they keep their shine for years. They are also malleable and ductile, allowing intricate designs.

Answer: A — Iron - metal; Sulphur - non-metal

Q46. medium

Silicon is important in the electronics industry because it is:

  1. A A semiconductor - it conducts electricity partially
  2. B A perfect conductor
  3. C A perfect insulator
  4. D A liquid metal

Answer: A — A semiconductor - it conducts electricity partially

Silicon's conductivity lies between that of metals and non-metals and can be controlled precisely, which makes it the basis of computer chips and solar cells.

SiliconSemiconductor

Q48. medium

Why are cooking vessel handles NOT made of metal?

  1. A Metals conduct heat well and would burn the hand
  2. B Metals are too light
  3. C Metals are transparent
  4. D Metals are cheap

Answer: A — Metals conduct heat well and would burn the hand

A metal handle would quickly become as hot as the pan. Wood and plastic are poor conductors (insulators), so they stay cool enough to hold.

ConductivityInsulators

Q50. medium

  1. A Mercury
  2. B Iron
  3. C Zinc

Answer: A — Mercury

Mercury expands evenly with temperature, does not stick to glass and is easy to see. Because it is toxic, digital and alcohol thermometers are now preferred.

MercuryThermometer

Q52. medium

  1. A Tin
  2. B Sodium

  4. D Potassium

Answer: A — Tin

'Tin' cans are actually steel cans coated with a thin layer of tin. Tin does not react with food and protects the iron underneath from rusting.

TinFood cans

Q54. medium

Nitrogen gas is filled in packets of chips because it:

  1. A Is unreactive and prevents the food from getting oxidised and spoiled
  2. B Makes the chips crispier chemically
  3. C Is heavier than air
  4. D Adds flavour

Answer: A — Is unreactive and prevents the food from getting oxidised and spoiled

Oxygen would react with the oils in chips and turn them rancid. Nitrogen is chemically inactive, so it keeps the food fresh and also cushions the chips.

NitrogenFood packaging

Answer: A — A non-metal that is liquid at room temperature

Bromine is the only non-metal that is liquid at room temperature - a reddish-brown, strong-smelling liquid. Mercury is the corresponding liquid metal.

Q58. medium

  2. B A sulphur lump

Copper is a metal with free electrons and conducts electricity well. Sulphur, rubber and wood are insulators (graphite is the notable non-metal exception).

Q60. medium

Why is copper used at the base of many stainless steel cooking pans?

  1. A Copper conducts heat better, so the food heats evenly and quickly
  2. B Copper is lighter than steel
  3. C Copper is cheaper than steel
  4. D Copper does not conduct heat at all

Answer: A — Copper conducts heat better, so the food heats evenly and quickly

Q24. hard

Do non-metals generally react with dilute acids to release hydrogen?

  1. A No - non-metals do not displace hydrogen from acids
  2. B Yes, all of them do
  3. C Only sulphur does
  4. D Only carbon does

Answer: A — No - non-metals do not displace hydrogen from acids

Non-metals cannot give away electrons to hydrogen ions, so they do not displace hydrogen from acids. This is a key difference from metals.

Q26. hard

Which metals do NOT react with water even at high temperatures?

  1. A Copper, silver and gold
  2. B Sodium and potassium
  3. C Calcium and magnesium
  4. D Iron and zinc

Answer: A — Copper, silver and gold

Copper, silver and gold are among the least reactive metals and do not react with water or steam. This is why they are used for ornaments, utensils and coins.

Q27. hard

The list of metals arranged in order of decreasing reactivity is called the:

  1. A Reactivity series
  2. B Periodic table
  3. C pH scale
  4. D Solubility chart

Answer: A — Reactivity series

In the reactivity series, potassium, sodium and calcium are most reactive, while gold and platinum are least reactive. A metal higher in the series can displace one lower down.

Reactivity series

Q29. hard

Copper cannot displace zinc from zinc sulphate solution because:

  1. A Copper is LESS reactive than zinc
  2. B Copper is more reactive
  3. C Zinc sulphate is insoluble
  4. D Copper is a non-metal

Answer: A — Copper is LESS reactive than zinc

Displacement works only downward in the reactivity series. Since zinc is above copper, copper cannot push zinc out of its salt - no reaction takes place.

Reactivity seriesNo reaction

Alloys are made to improve properties. Steel (iron + carbon), brass (copper + zinc) and bronze (copper + tin) are common alloys, stronger or more corrosion-resistant than the pure metals.

Q59. hard

Which overall statement about metals and non-metals is CORRECT?

  1. A Metals are lustrous, malleable, ductile, sonorous conductors that form basic oxides; non-metals are mostly dull, brittle non-conductors that form acidic oxides
  2. B Metals and non-metals have identical properties
  3. C Non-metals are always shinier than metals
  4. D Only metals exist as gases

Answer: A — Metals are lustrous, malleable, ductile, sonorous conductors that form basic oxides; non-metals are mostly dull, brittle non-conductors that form acidic oxides

This sums up the topic. Remember the exceptions too: mercury (liquid metal), sodium and potassium (soft metals), diamond (hard non-metal), graphite (conducting non-metal) and bromine (liquid non-metal).
