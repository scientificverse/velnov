Easy questions (2)

Q1. easy

Air is:

  1. A A mixture of gases, water vapour and dust particles
  2. B A pure single gas
  3. C A compound of oxygen and nitrogen
  4. D An element

Answer: A — A mixture of gases, water vapour and dust particles

Air is a mixture, not a compound. Its components keep their own properties and can be separated by physical means such as fractional distillation of liquid air.

AirMixture

Q2. easy

Which gas is present in the LARGEST amount in air?

  1. A Nitrogen (about 78%)
  2. B Oxygen
  3. C Carbon dioxide
  4. D Argon

Answer: A — Nitrogen (about 78%)

Nitrogen makes up about 78% of air by volume, oxygen about 21%, and the remaining 1% is argon, carbon dioxide, water vapour and other gases.

Air compositionNitrogen

Oxygen makes up roughly 21% of air. It supports respiration and combustion, and its level is maintained by photosynthesis in plants.

Answer: A — About 0.03-0.04%

Carbon dioxide is only a tiny fraction of air, but it is essential for photosynthesis and, in excess, is the main driver of global warming.

Answer: A — Burning of coal and fuels containing sulphur, in power plants and refineries

Answer: A — Corrodes the marble (calcium carbonate), making it yellow and pitted

Acidic pollutants from the nearby Mathura refinery and industries react with the marble, corroding and discolouring it - a phenomenon called 'marble cancer'.

Q12. medium

The greenhouse effect is:

  1. A The trapping of the Earth's heat by gases like carbon dioxide in the atmosphere
  2. B The growing of plants in glass houses only
  3. C The cooling of the Earth
  4. D A type of rainfall

Answer: A — The trapping of the Earth's heat by gases like carbon dioxide in the atmosphere

Greenhouse gases let sunlight in but absorb the heat radiated back by the Earth. A natural greenhouse effect keeps the planet habitable, but excess gases cause harmful warming.

Greenhouse effect

  1. A Global warming

  3. C Ozone depletion
  4. D Smog

Answer: A — Global warming

Global warming causes melting of glaciers and polar ice, rising sea levels, changed rainfall patterns and more extreme weather.

Global warming

Q16. medium

  4. D Frost

Smog forms in cold, humid weather when smoke particles mix with fog. It causes breathing difficulties and reduces visibility, disrupting traffic and flights.

Smog

Q18. medium

CFCs used in refrigerators and air conditioners damage:

  1. A The ozone layer
  2. B The soil only
  3. C Rivers only

Chlorofluorocarbons break down ozone molecules in the stratosphere, thinning the ozone layer that shields us from harmful ultraviolet radiation.

Q28. medium

Answer: A — Thermal pollution, which harms fish and aquatic organisms

Warm water holds less dissolved oxygen and disturbs the breeding and survival of aquatic species. This is called thermal pollution.

Thermal pollution

Q30. medium

  2. B Adding sugar
  3. C Freezing it

Answer: A — Boiling it for several minutes to kill germs

Boiling kills bacteria, viruses and parasites. It is the cheapest and most reliable way to make water microbiologically safe at home.

BoilingWater purification

Q32. medium

Which method of water purification uses ultraviolet radiation?

  1. A UV filters, which kill germs without adding any chemicals
  2. B Boiling
  3. C Candle filtration
  4. D Decantation

Answer: A — UV filters, which kill germs without adding any chemicals

Ultraviolet light damages the DNA of micro-organisms so they cannot multiply. UV purifiers work quickly and leave no taste or chemical residue.

Q34. medium

Answer: A — Delivers water drop by drop directly to the roots, reducing wastage

Narrow pipes with tiny holes drip water at the base of each plant. Very little is lost to evaporation or runoff, so crops need far less water.

Drip irrigationWater conservation

Aquatic animals breathe oxygen dissolved in the water through their gills. Pollution and warm water reduce dissolved oxygen and can suffocate fish.

Q56. medium

The percentage of oxygen in air stays roughly constant because of:

  1. A The balance between respiration/combustion and photosynthesis by plants
  2. B Wind mixing the air
  3. C Rainfall
  4. D Nothing in particular

Answer: A — The balance between respiration/combustion and photosynthesis by plants

Animals and burning consume oxygen and release carbon dioxide; plants do the reverse during photosynthesis. This oxygen-carbon dioxide cycle keeps the levels stable - which is why deforestation is so damaging.

Oxygen cyclePhotosynthesis

  1. A Air pollution

Air pollution occurs when gases, smoke and particles harmful to living beings are added to air. The substances that cause it are called air pollutants.

  1. A Carbon monoxide
  2. B Oxygen
  3. C Nitrogen
  4. D Argon

Answer: A — Carbon monoxide

Carbon monoxide, produced by incomplete combustion in vehicles and stoves, binds strongly to haemoglobin, preventing it from carrying oxygen. It can be fatal.

Carbon monoxidePollutant

Q9. hard

  1. A Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides
  2. B Oxygen and nitrogen
  3. C Helium and argon
  4. D Methane only

Answer: A — Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides

SO2 and NOx dissolve in atmospheric moisture to form sulphuric and nitric acids, which fall as acid rain and damage crops, soil, water bodies and monuments.

Q27. hard

Excess fertilisers in a lake cause excessive growth of algae. This is called:

  3. C Chlorination
  4. D Sedimentation

Q33. hard
