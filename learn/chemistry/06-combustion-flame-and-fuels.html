Easy questions (2)

Q1. easy

A chemical process in which a substance reacts with oxygen to give off heat is called:

  1. A Combustion
  2. B Condensation
  3. C Evaporation
  4. D Sublimation

Answer: A — Combustion

Combustion is the reaction of a substance with oxygen, releasing heat and often light. Burning of wood, coal and LPG are all combustion reactions.

Combustion

Q2. easy

A substance that burns in air producing heat and light is called:

  1. A A combustible substance
  2. B A non-combustible substance
  3. C An indicator
  4. D A catalyst

Answer: A — A combustible substance

Combustible substances such as paper, wood, LPG and kerosene burn in air. Non-combustible substances like stone, glass and iron nails do not.

Combustible substances

Medium questions (30)

Q3. medium

Which three conditions are necessary for combustion?

  1. A A combustible substance, a supporter of combustion (oxygen), and the ignition temperature
  2. B Water, air and soil
  3. C Light, sound and heat
  4. D Carbon dioxide, nitrogen and water

Answer: A — A combustible substance, a supporter of combustion (oxygen), and the ignition temperature

All three must be present at once. Removing any one of them - fuel, oxygen or heat - stops the burning, which is the basis of all fire fighting.

Conditions for combustionFire triangle

Q4. medium

The lowest temperature at which a substance catches fire is called its:

  1. A Ignition temperature
  2. B Melting point
  3. C Boiling point
  4. D Freezing point

Answer: A — Ignition temperature

A substance will not burn until it reaches its ignition temperature, no matter how much oxygen is available. Kerosene oil has a low ignition temperature; coal has a high one.

Ignition temperature

Oxygen is the supporter of combustion. A burning candle goes out when covered by a glass because the oxygen inside is used up.

OxygenSupporter of combustion

Answer: A — The supply of oxygen is cut off

Once the limited oxygen inside the glass is consumed, combustion cannot continue and the flame dies. This shows oxygen is essential for burning.

Answer: A — It undergoes spontaneous combustion in air at room temperature

Complete combustion occurs in the outer zone where oxygen is plentiful, so it is the hottest. Goldsmiths direct this part of the flame onto gold to melt it.

Q39. medium

When CO2 is released from a cylinder it also helps put out fire because it:

  1. A Expands and cools, lowering the temperature below the ignition point
  2. B Heats the fuel
  3. C Adds oxygen
  4. D Adds fuel

Answer: A — Expands and cools, lowering the temperature below the ignition point

As compressed CO2 escapes it expands rapidly and cools sharply. This drop in temperature, along with the oxygen cut-off, extinguishes the fire.

Carbon dioxideCooling

Q58. medium

Why does a candle flame always point upwards, whichever way the candle is tilted?

  1. A Hot gases are lighter and rise, while cooler air moves in from below
  2. B The wax pushes the flame up
  3. C Light always travels upwards
  4. D Oxygen is found only above the flame

Answer: A — Hot gases are lighter and rise, while cooler air moves in from below

The hot gases produced by burning are less dense than the surrounding air, so they rise. Cool air rushes in at the bottom to replace them, giving the flame its familiar upward teardrop shape.

Q60. medium

  2. B Adds fuel to the fire

  4. D Makes the fire burn faster

Answer: A — Cuts off the oxygen supply and also cools the burning material

The blanket smothers the fire by keeping air away, and the water in it absorbs heat, lowering the temperature below the ignition point - two of the three fire-fighting methods at once.

Q5. hard

Substances with a VERY LOW ignition temperature that catch fire easily are called:

  1. A Inflammable substances
  2. B Non-combustible substances
  3. C Fire extinguishers
  4. D Insulators

Answer: A — Inflammable substances

Petrol, alcohol, LPG and spirit have very low ignition temperatures and catch fire easily, so they are called inflammable (or flammable) substances and must be handled with care.

Inflammable substances

  2. B Iron has no mass

Every substance has its own ignition temperature. Paper reaches its ignition temperature quickly in a small flame, while iron would need a far higher temperature.

Ignition temperatureComparison

Q59. hard
