Topic overview

Coal, Petroleum and Natural Resources – using what nature gives

In this overview: Natural resources · Fossil fuels · Coal · Petroleum · Natural gas · Where they are found in India · Problems and conservation · Common misconceptions · Quick revision · Test your understanding

1. Natural resources

Natural resources are things found in nature that people use.

  • Inexhaustible resources are present in unlimited amounts and will not run out, e.g. sunlight and air.
  • Exhaustible resources are limited and can be used up, e.g. forests, minerals, coal, petroleum and natural gas.

2. Fossil fuels

Fossil fuels are fuels formed from the remains of dead plants and animals buried under the earth for millions of years.

Coal (solid) · Petroleum (liquid) · Natural gas (gas)

Remember: Fossil fuels take millions of years to form, so once used up they cannot be replaced in our lifetime.

3. Coal

Coal formed from huge forests that were buried about 300 million years ago. Heat and pressure, without air, slowly turned the plants into coal. This process is called carbonisation.

Coal is mostly carbon. Its grades, from lowest to highest carbon content, are:

Peat → Lignite → Bituminous coal → Anthracite (best)

Example: Most of India's electricity comes from thermal power plants, which burn coal to turn water into steam that spins turbines.

Products from coal

Heating coal without air (destructive distillation) gives three useful products:

  • Coke: almost pure carbon, tough and porous. Used to make steel and to extract metals.
  • Coal tar: a thick, black, smelly liquid that is a mixture of about 200 substances. Used to make dyes, paints, drugs, plastics, perfumes and naphthalene balls.
  • Coal gas: a fuel gas. It lit the streets of London from 1810, and is now used in industry.

4. Petroleum

Petroleum formed from tiny sea plants and animals that died, sank to the sea bed and were covered with sand and clay. Over millions of years, heat and pressure turned them into oil and natural gas.

Oil and gas collect in rock, trapped under a layer that does not let them through. Gas sits on top, then oil, then water.

Examples:

  • The world's first oil well was drilled in Pennsylvania, USA, in 1859.
  • India's first oil well was at Digboi, Assam, in 1867.
  • Petroleum is called black gold because it is dark and very valuable.

Refining

Crude oil is a mixture, so it must be refined before use. It is separated in a refinery by fractional distillation, because each part (fraction) has a different boiling point.

Lightest (top) → Petroleum gas · Petrol · Kerosene · Diesel · Lubricating oil · Paraffin wax · Bitumen ← Heaviest (bottom)

Uses of petroleum products

  • Petroleum gas (LPG): cooking fuel.
  • Petrol: cars and motorcycles; also a dry-cleaning solvent.
  • Kerosene: jet aircraft fuel, stoves and lamps.
  • Diesel: trucks, buses, tractors and generators.
  • Lubricating oil: reduces friction in machines.
  • Paraffin wax: candles, ointments and vaseline.
  • Bitumen: road surfaces and waterproof paints.

Petrochemicals from petroleum and natural gas are used to make plastics, polythene, nylon, polyester and detergents.

5. Natural gas

Natural gas is found with petroleum. It is mainly methane. Stored under high pressure, it is called CNG (Compressed Natural Gas).

  • It is a clean fuel, with very little smoke.
  • It can be sent straight to homes and factories through pipelines.
  • It supplies hydrogen for making fertilisers.

Example: Delhi's buses and autorickshaws switched to CNG to cut air pollution.

6. Where they are found in India

  • Coal: Jharkhand, Odisha, West Bengal, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh.
  • Petroleum: Assam, Gujarat, Maharashtra (Mumbai High) and Rajasthan.
  • Natural gas: Tripura, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, and the Krishna–Godavari delta.

7. Problems and conservation

Fossil fuels are limited, and burning them causes pollution: carbon dioxide (global warming), sulphur dioxide (acid rain) and soot.

Saving fuel (PCRA tips)

The Petroleum Conservation Research Association (PCRA) advises:

  • Drive at a steady, moderate speed.
  • Switch off the engine at traffic lights.
  • Keep the tyre pressure correct, and service vehicles regularly.

Alternatives

Renewable energy sources do not run out, e.g. solar, wind, water (hydro) and biogas.

Example: Biogas is made when bacteria break down cow dung and plant waste without air. It is mostly methane, and the leftover slurry is good manure.

Petroleum cannot be used directly as a fuel because:

  1. A Crude oil is a mixture of many substances that must first be separated by refining
  2. B It is too light
  3. C It does not burn
  4. D It is a solid

Answer: A — Crude oil is a mixture of many substances that must first be separated by refining

Crude petroleum contains gases, petrol, kerosene, diesel, oils and wax all mixed together. Each has a different use, so refining is necessary before it can be used.

  2. B Bitumen, diesel, petrol, petroleum gas

Answer: A — Petroleum gas, petrol, kerosene, diesel, lubricating oil, bitumen

Boiling point rises down the list. Petroleum gas boils below room temperature; bitumen is a thick residue that hardly boils at all.

Q53. hard

Why is the rate of formation of fossil fuels irrelevant to conservation planning?

  1. A They take millions of years to form, so on any human timescale they cannot be replaced
  2. B They form every year
  3. C They form in a decade
  4. D They are made in factories

Answer: A — They take millions of years to form, so on any human timescale they cannot be replaced

Even though fossil fuels are still forming somewhere, the process takes hundreds of millions of years. We use them millions of times faster than they form, so they are effectively non-renewable.

Q55. hard

Which is the highest-quality coal, with the greatest carbon content?

  1. A Anthracite
  2. B Peat
  3. C Lignite
  4. D Bituminous

Answer: A — Anthracite

Coal quality improves in the order peat, lignite, bituminous, anthracite. Anthracite has the highest carbon content, burns with little smoke and gives the most heat.

Thermal power plants in India use coal mainly to:

  1. A Heat water into steam that turns turbines and generates electricity
  2. B Make plastics
  3. C Purify water
  4. D Make fertilisers only

Answer: A — Heat water into steam that turns turbines and generates electricity

Burning coal boils water into high-pressure steam. The steam spins a turbine coupled to a generator, producing electricity. Most of India's electricity still comes from coal.

Q60. hard

Why are petroleum refineries usually built near ports or oil fields?

  1. A To reduce the cost and risk of transporting large quantities of crude oil
  2. B Because refining needs sea water
  3. C Because crude oil floats
  4. D For better scenery

Answer: A — To reduce the cost and risk of transporting large quantities of crude oil

Crude oil arrives in huge tankers or through pipelines. Siting refineries at ports or oil fields cuts transport costs and the risk of spills, and the lighter refined products are then distributed onward.
