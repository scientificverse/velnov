Easy questions (4)

Q1. easy

An adjective is a word that describes a:

  1. A Noun (or pronoun)
  2. B Verb
  3. C Place only
  4. D Number only

Answer: A — Noun (or pronoun)

An adjective is a describing word. It tells us more about a noun - its size, colour, shape or number, like 'a big house' or 'a red ball'.

Q2. easy

  1. A tall
  2. B tree

  4. D none

Answer: A — tall

'Tall' describes the tree - it tells us about its height, so it is the adjective. 'Tree' is the noun.

Q13. hard

Adjectives that compare TWO things and often end in '-er' are in the:

  1. A Comparative degree
  2. B Positive degree
  3. C Superlative degree
  4. D Past degree

Answer: A — Comparative degree

The comparative degree compares two things and usually ends in '-er' or uses 'more' - taller, bigger, more beautiful.

DegreesComparative

Q14. hard

Adjectives that compare THREE or more things and often end in '-est' are in the:

  1. A Superlative degree
  2. B Comparative degree
  3. C Positive degree
  4. D Future degree

Answer: A — Superlative degree

The superlative degree shows the highest degree among three or more, usually ending in '-est' or using 'most' - tallest, biggest, most beautiful.

DegreesSuperlative

Q20. hard

What is the comparative form of 'big'?

  1. A bigger
  2. B biger
  3. C more big
  4. D bigest

Answer: A — bigger

For short words ending in a single vowel + consonant, we double the last letter and add '-er': big → bigger, hot → hotter, thin → thinner.

DegreesDouble letter

Q21. hard

  1. A happier
  2. B happyer
  3. C more happy
  4. D happiest

Answer: A — happier

For adjectives ending in a consonant + 'y', we change 'y' to 'i' and add '-er': happy → happier, easy → easier.

Q23. hard

For long adjectives like 'beautiful', how do we make the comparative?

Q58. hard

Which overall statement about adjectives is CORRECT?

  1. A An adjective describes a noun, telling its size, colour, shape, number or quality, and has three degrees: positive, comparative and superlative
  2. B An adjective describes a verb
  3. C Adjectives never change form
  4. D An adjective names a person

Answer: A — An adjective describes a noun, telling its size, colour, shape, number or quality, and has three degrees: positive, comparative and superlative

This sums up the topic: adjectives are describing words; they can tell size, colour, shape, number and quality, and they change form to compare - positive (tall), comparative (taller) and superlative (tallest).
