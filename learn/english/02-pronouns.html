Easy questions (7)

Q1. easy

A pronoun is a word used in place of a:

  1. A Noun
  2. B Verb
  3. C Colour
  4. D Number

Answer: A — Noun

A pronoun takes the place of a noun so we do not have to repeat it. Instead of 'Ravi ran, Ravi fell', we say 'Ravi ran, he fell'.

Pronouns

Q2. easy

Which of these is a pronoun?

  1. A she
  2. B table
  3. C run
  4. D happy

Answer: A — she

'She' is a pronoun - it can stand for a girl or woman. 'Table' is a noun, 'run' a verb and 'happy' an adjective.

Q4. easy

Which pronoun do we use for a BOY or man?

  1. A he
  2. B she
  3. C it
  4. D they

Answer: A — he

We use 'he' for a single male - a boy or a man. For a female we use 'she', and for a thing or animal we use 'it'.

Q14. hard

The pronoun 'I' should always be written:

  1. A As a capital letter, even in the middle of a sentence
  2. B As a small letter
  3. C Only at the start
  4. D With a full stop

Answer: A — As a capital letter, even in the middle of a sentence

The pronoun 'I' is always a capital letter wherever it appears - 'My friend and I went home.'

Q16. hard

Which of these words are ALL pronouns?

  1. A I, you, he, she, it, we, they
  2. B run, jump, sit, walk
  3. C big, small, tall, short
  4. D cat, dog, book, pen

Answer: A — I, you, he, she, it, we, they

I, you, he, she, it, we and they are the common personal pronouns. The other lists are verbs, adjectives and nouns.

Q58. hard

Which overall statement about pronouns is CORRECT?

  1. A A pronoun is used in place of a noun; examples are I, you, he, she, it, we and they, along with possessives like my, his and their
  2. B A pronoun names an action
  3. C Pronouns are always nouns
  4. D Every sentence needs three pronouns

Answer: A — A pronoun is used in place of a noun; examples are I, you, he, she, it, we and they, along with possessives like my, his and their

This sums up the topic: pronouns replace nouns to avoid repeating them; we choose them by gender and number (he, she, it, they) and use possessive forms (my, his, her, its, our, their) to show ownership.
